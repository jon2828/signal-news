A federal court on March 14, 2025 rejected Elon Musk's request for a preliminary injunction against OpenAI. The judge said Musk had not shown a "likelihood of success on the merits" and tossed several of his claims outright. That decision pushes the lawsuit closer to dismissal.

Musk's legal strategy has been clear: he wants to slow OpenAI while he builds his own for‑profit AI venture, xAI. The court's ruling shows that his arguments don't hold up under scrutiny. OpenAI's filing notes that the non‑profit arm is staying put, contrary to Musk's claim that a "conversion" is imminent. The company says it will keep the non‑profit as a core part of its mission and give it a sizable stake in a proposed public‑benefit corporation.

The backstory is messy. In 2017, Musk pushed for a $1 billion funding commitment from the OpenAI non‑profit, even offering to cover any shortfall ,  but the non‑profit raised less than $45 million from Musk and over $90 million from other donors 【2†source】. When the founders realized that building AGI would need billions of dollars per year, they agreed a for‑profit entity was necessary. Musk wanted majority equity, board control, and the CEO seat, but OpenAI balked at giving any individual absolute control 【2†source】.

Musk then suggested merging OpenAI into Tesla, calling it "the only path that could even hope to hold a candle to Google" 【2†source】. OpenAI turned that down, and Musk left in early 2018, saying the probability of success was zero. He later warned that "several hundred million won't be enough" and that the effort needed "billions per year" 【2†source】.

Since then, OpenAI has kept building widely available tools. Hundreds of millions of users access a free tier, and governments and NGOs are using the tech for public‑good projects, from speeding up Albania's EU accession to cutting agricultural extension costs in Kenya by a factor of 100. Those outcomes make Musk's claim that OpenAI is "failing" look increasingly hollow.

The court's decision also sends a signal to other would‑be litigants: baseless suits aimed at stalling competition will be scrutinized closely. OpenAI's legal team emphasized that the non‑profit will not be sold or weakened; instead, it will be "strengthened" and hold a "significant stake" in the new public‑benefit corporation 【1†source】. That language mirrors Musk's own 2017 comment that OpenAI needed to evolve its structure, but it flips the narrative, OpenAI is evolving on its own terms, not under Musk's control.

I keep coming back to the numbers. Musk's $1 billion pledge never materialized, while OpenAI has pulled in over $135 million from a mix of donors and bridge funding from Reid Hoffman. The court's rejection means Musk can't force OpenAI to hand over any of that capital or to merge with his companies. It also means the lawsuit's remaining claims will likely be dismissed in the coming months.

Nobody knows how this will play out in the long run. Musk may double down on xAI, or he may find another legal angle. For now, the court has drawn a line: OpenAI's mission stays intact, its non‑profit arm remains, and the lawsuit is on thin ice.
