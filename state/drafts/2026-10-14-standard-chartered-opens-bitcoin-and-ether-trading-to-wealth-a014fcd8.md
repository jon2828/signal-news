Standard Chartered is now letting wealthy clients in the United Arab Emirates buy and sell bitcoin and ether directly through their existing bank accounts, the first offering of its kind from an international bank in the country, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/14/standard-chartered-unlocks-direct-bitcoin-and-ether-trading-for-affluent-uae-clients).

The service is limited to professional investors. Per [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/standard-chartered-bitcoin-ether-trading-professional-investors-uae), that means individuals with at least 4 million dirhams (about $1.1 million) in net assets, excluding their main residence, plus sufficient investment knowledge and experience, or representation by a licensed entity.

## How it works

The bank handles custody and settlement in-house through what it calls a closed-loop model. Clients never touch a crypto wallet or a third-party exchange. Trading runs through Zodia Markets, the venue backed by Standard Chartered's SC Ventures unit, and the whole operation sits under a license from Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA).

This is also a first for the bank itself. Standard Chartered says it has never offered crypto trading directly to wealthy clients in any market before. It launched institutional bitcoin and ether spot trading through its UK arm in July of last year, then expanded that institutional service to the UAE on Sept. 3.

Samir Subberwal, the bank's global head of wealth solutions, said digital assets are "moving into mainstream wealth conversations" and that clients want the "security, trust and confidence that comes from investing through a regulated and trusted institution."

## My take

I keep coming back to the closed-loop part. On one hand, this is real. A major international bank, a regulatory license, bitcoin and ether sitting next to someone's deposit accounts. Five years ago that sentence would have read like parody.

On the other hand, the design strips out the thing that makes bitcoin bitcoin. No wallet means the coins stay inside the bank's ecosystem, at least as the announcement describes it. You get price exposure. The bank keeps the keys, collects the fees, and owns the relationship. What's being sold here is bitcoin as a line item on a statement, not bitcoin as an exit from the banking system.

That may be exactly what these clients want. If you have $1.1 million in net assets and a private banker, you probably don't want to manage seed phrases. Fair enough. But the gap between "bank sells you bitcoin exposure" and "you own bitcoin" is worth naming, because the marketing will not name it.

The wider UAE picture matters too. Kraken's parent company Payward received VARA approval on Monday, and neobank Revolut picked up in-principle approval in July, per Cointelegraph. Dubai is issuing licenses at a steady clip, and banks and exchanges are racing to become the default on-ramp for wealthy Gulf money. Standard Chartered got there first among international banks.

Whether closed-loop crypto counts as adoption or as an expensive wrapper, I can't say cleanly. Probably both, depending on who's buying.
