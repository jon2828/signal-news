Web3Auth says it beat hackers to the punch, moving $5.6 million out of exposed Keplr wallets before the funds could be stolen, [according to The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-08-web3auth-says-it-frontran-hackers-by-moving-5-6-million-out-of-exposed-keplr-wallets-436029).

That is nearly everything we know. The report is thin, and the most interesting detail in it is a two-word phrase. Web3Auth's communications referred to an "original attacker," which suggests at least some funds were stolen before the rescue. The company, per The Block, "did not elaborate."

## Frontrunning, but defensively

Frontrunning is normally something done to traders, not for them. The idea here, as Web3Auth tells it, is that the company spotted the exposed wallets and swept the funds to safety before thieves could drain them. If that account holds up, it is the rare case where the trick gets used as a shield.

But "says" is doing a lot of work in that first sentence. This is the company's own version of events. The source material has no independent confirmation, no on-chain breakdown, no postmortem. Just the claim and the number.

## The questions that matter

How were the wallets exposed in the first place, and for how long? How much did the "original attacker" actually take before Web3Auth moved? Where is the $5.6 million sitting now, and when do users get it back?

None of that is in the report.

## My read

I keep coming back to that phrase, "original attacker." It tells you the timeline. Someone got there first, took something, and then Web3Auth moved the rest. A company in that position has every incentive to lead with the heroic half of the story, the $5.6 million saved, and stay quiet about the part that walked out the door.

Maybe the full story is just as clean as the headline. Maybe it isn't. White-hat fund rescues are a real pattern in crypto, and this could be a good one. It could also be a hack with a well-dressed press strategy.

Until there is a postmortem or on-chain evidence, treat the $5.6 million as a claim, not a fact.
