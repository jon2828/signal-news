Patrick Witt, the White House's crypto chief, announced his departure from the White House last week, according to [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/18/the-white-house-s-crypto-chief-departs-state-of-crypto).

That is, honestly, most of the story. The report ran in CoinDesk's State of Crypto newsletter, dated October 18, and the summary available to us gives no reason for the exit, names no successor, and offers no timeline beyond "last week." I'd rather tell you that plainly than pretend there's more here.

## What we don't know

Start with the basics. We don't know why Witt is leaving. We don't know whether he resigned, was pushed, or finished a stint that was always meant to be short. We don't know who replaces him, or whether the job continues in its current form at all.

I'm not going to guess at any of that. A short summary is a short summary. Inflating it with speculation would waste your time, and this site doesn't do that.

## Why it's still worth a post

Because personnel is policy, especially at this level. The crypto chief job is, as far as I can tell, the industry's most direct line into the executive branch. Whoever holds it influences which ideas get a hearing and which ones stall out. When the seat changes hands, every live conversation in Washington crypto policy gets a new person across the table. Or an empty chair.

People leave White House jobs all the time, so turnover by itself isn't a scandal or even much of a signal. The signal comes from what happens next. A quick successor suggests the administration wants continuity on crypto. A long vacancy, or a restructured role, would say something else.

The things worth watching from here: a formal announcement, a named replacement, any statement from Witt himself. Any one of those turns this from a one-line item into an actual story.

For now, the honest version is this. The White House's crypto chief is on his way out, and nobody outside the building knows what happens next. When a name or a reason surfaces, there will be more to say. Until then, there isn't.
