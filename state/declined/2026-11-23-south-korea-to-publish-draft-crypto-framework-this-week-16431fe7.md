South Korea will publish a draft of its comprehensive crypto legal framework this week, The Block reports. The bill, which The Block identifies as the Digital Asset Basic Act, is expected to include a licensing regime for crypto businesses and a bank-centered issuance system for local stablecoins.

That is the sum of what's public right now. A draft, due within days, with two known pillars. Everything else is waiting.

## What the draft is expected to contain

Two pieces, per The Block's summary. First, licenses for crypto businesses. Second, banks at the center of stablecoin issuance.

The licensing piece is the least surprising thing a comprehensive crypto bill can contain. You want to run a crypto business, you get a license, you answer to a regulator. It's the default tool, and I'd have been shocked if it weren't in there.

The stablecoin piece is where it gets interesting.

## Banks as the stablecoin gatekeepers

A "bank-centered issuance system" is a design choice, and a revealing one. It says the people drafting this law trust banks to issue local stablecoins, or at least trust them more than they trust crypto-native firms.

My read: if banks are the issuers, every crypto company that wants a piece of the stablecoin business in Korea ends up either building on top of bank-issued tokens or getting out of the way. That is a very different market from one where any licensed firm can issue. It hands the most profitable part of the stablecoin stack to the sector regulators already supervise closely, which is probably the point.

I keep coming back to the word "centered." Centered is not "exclusive." The draft might let non-bank firms issue through bank partnerships, or with banks holding the reserves, or it might lock issuance to banks entirely. The summary doesn't say, and the gap between those outcomes is enormous.

## What we don't know

Almost everything else. We don't know whether the licensing regime is strict or permissive, what it costs, who counts as a crypto business, or what happens to firms already operating in the country. We don't know if the bank-centered model is a hard rule or a default with exceptions. The draft will answer some of this when it lands this week, and then the amendment fight begins.

I'll admit my bias here. I'm skeptical of comprehensive frameworks that arrive with banks already installed at the center of the new system, because bills get negotiated, and negotiated bills tend to get friendlier to incumbents with each round. Starting from "banks issue the stablecoins" suggests the incumbents are winning before the debate even starts.

That's speculation, clearly labeled. What the reporting supports: the draft is coming this week, it covers licensing and stablecoins, and banks sit in the middle of the stablecoin plan. The rest is a few days away.
