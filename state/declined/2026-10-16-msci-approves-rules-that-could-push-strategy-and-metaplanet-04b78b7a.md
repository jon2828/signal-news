MSCI has approved rules that could push Strategy and Metaplanet out of its equity indexes, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/16/msci-approves-rules-that-could-eject-strategy-metaplanet-from-equity-indexes). The rules, first proposed in August, would make companies that exist mainly to hold investments ineligible for index inclusion.

## What the rule says

The CoinDesk item is short, but the core of it is clear. MSCI floated the change in August and has now signed off on it. If a company's main business is holding investments, it no longer qualifies for the firm's equity indexes. Strategy and Metaplanet are the two names CoinDesk puts at risk of ejection.

Read that definition plainly and it describes the bitcoin treasury model. That is why a crypto outlet is covering an index provider's rule change, and why these two companies are the ones in the headline.

## Why this is a real threat

Index membership is plumbing, not prestige. Funds that track an index have to hold what is in it and sell what leaves. If MSCI drops a stock, every passive product tracking that index becomes a seller on a fixed schedule, no opinion required. That kind of selling is mechanical, and mechanical selling is hard to argue with.

The treasury model makes the exposure worse. A company whose main asset is its own holdings depends on steady demand for its stock, and index membership is a big part of what keeps that demand deep. The appeal of the structure is a feedback loop: strong share demand lets the company raise money and grow its holdings, which feeds the story again. Ejection attacks the first step of that loop. I read this rule as a hit on the structure itself, not on any one company's choices.

There is also a power question worth sitting with. A neutral-sounding test, companies that "exist mainly to hold investments," in CoinDesk's words, ends up deciding which business models count as index-worthy. Plenty of large companies hold big investment portfolios alongside operating businesses. Where MSCI draws that line will matter well beyond bitcoin.

## What we don't know

Almost everything that decides the impact. The summary does not say when the rules take effect or how MSCI will define "mainly." It also does not say whether a company can restructure its way back to eligibility, or how current index members get treated while the change phases in.

Until those details surface, this is a warning shot rather than an execution. But it comes from the firm whose lists decide what the world's passive funds have to own. The treasury trade ignores that at its own risk.
