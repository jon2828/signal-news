Binance Pool is shutting down, and the miners still pointing machines at it have until November 10 to find another provider. CoinDesk reports the pool's hashrate has fallen 65%, from 26 EH/s in December to about 9 EH/s.

## A leak, not a collapse

The shape of this decline matters more than the headline. A pool that once handled 2.3% of the Bitcoin network's computing power lost two-thirds of its hashrate in the months since December. That's not a collapse. It's an exodus.

When a pool dies overnight, something broke: a hack, a legal order. When it bleeds steadily for the better part of a year, the cause is usually duller and more damning. Individual operators ran the numbers and decided they were better off somewhere else, one by one, until there was nothing left worth keeping open. I find that kind of quiet verdict more interesting than any dramatic failure.

## What 2.3% actually means

For Bitcoin itself, this is a non-event. The machines behind that 9 EH/s don't vanish when the pool closes. They point at a different pool and keep hashing, and the network carries on. A pool this size disappearing does not meaningfully touch security or block times.

For the miners involved, it's a genuine headache. Moving means reconfiguring hardware and setting up somewhere new, with whatever downtime the switch costs them. The November 10 cutoff turns a decision they may have been putting off into a hard deadline.

The question worth asking is where that hashrate lands. My guess, and it is only a guess, is that most of it ends up at larger pools. Operators who just watched one provider shrink tend to pick the option that looks permanent. If that's right, every shutdown like this nudges a bit more of the network's computing power into fewer hands. Whether that trend is actually playing out, the reporting here doesn't say. But it's the thing I'll be watching.

## What we don't know

Most of the story, frankly. The CoinDesk summary doesn't say why miners left Binance Pool. It doesn't say whether Binance chose to close the pool or simply accepted the inevitable. There's no statement quoted and no reason given. What happens to anyone still connected after the cutoff isn't addressed either.

A 65% decline has a cause. We just don't have it yet. If one surfaces, this gets a second post.
