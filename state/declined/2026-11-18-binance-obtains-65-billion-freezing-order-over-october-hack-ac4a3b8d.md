Binance has obtained a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts as it tries to recover crypto stolen in an October hack, according to The Block.

That is, almost word for word, the entire story right now. The Block's item is a short summary, and it raises more questions than it answers. But $6.5 billion is $6.5 billion, so it's worth a post.

## What we know

The facts fit in a paragraph. Binance went to the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts and came out with a worldwide freezing order valued at $6.5 billion. The order is part of an effort to recover funds taken in what the summary calls an "October hack." Nothing more specific is given about the hack itself.

What we don't know would fill a much longer paragraph. The summary doesn't name who the order targets or which assets are covered. It doesn't say how much was actually stolen in October, or whether $6.5 billion is the size of the theft or the value of the assets being frozen.

## The number

I keep coming back to the number. $6.5 billion is a huge figure even by the standards of an industry that has seen plenty of large thefts. If the October hack was anywhere near that size, it was a very serious breach. If the order instead covers assets well beyond what was stolen, that tells you something about how aggressively Binance is pursuing recovery. Either reading is speculation. The summary doesn't say, and I won't pretend it does.

## Freezing is not recovering

My read on this is simple. A freezing order is a court document, not a recovered coin. It has teeth only if the assets sit with someone who respects court orders, a regulated exchange or a custodian. If the attacker has already moved the funds through mixers or onto chains with no central party to serve papers on, the order is mostly symbolic. Big freezing orders make for big headlines. Actual recovery is slower and usually partial.

None of that is reporting on this case, to be clear. It's the general pattern with stolen crypto, and until more detail comes out, the general pattern is all any of us have to go on. The summary is thin enough that I'd rather flag the gaps than fill them with guesses.

Whether Binance ever gets this money back is an open question. Nobody outside the case knows how it plays out.
