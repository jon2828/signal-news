South Korea's draft crypto bill would create a path for domestic ICOs and cap any single shareholder in a crypto exchange at 20%, according to The Block.

The draft now heads to the legislature, where lawmakers will debate it and may fold it into competing proposals. That is most of what we know. The Block's item is a summary, not the bill text, so the fine print, who drafted it, what the ICO rules would actually require, is not public in what I have seen.

## Two provisions, one direction

The ICO piece is the headline. Letting domestic token sales happen under a Korean framework is a real policy choice. It says the government would rather have that activity inside its borders, under its rules, than pushed somewhere it cannot see. That is my read, not a quote from the bill.

The ownership cap is the stranger provision. A 20% ceiling on exchange shareholders is not something you write if you trust incumbents to behave. You write it because you are worried about concentrated control of the venues where people trade. Whether it works is another matter. Ownership caps can be gamed with related parties and friendly nominees, and I have no reason to think Korea's version would be immune.

## The ICO question

I keep coming back to the ICO part. An ICO is a promise sold before a product exists, and the buyer carries the risk that the promise is worth anything at all. If Korea wants to run that experiment domestically, under rules, that is a bet that disclosure requirements can make a risky fundraising model safe enough. I am not convinced. I am willing to be wrong.

## What happens next

Per The Block, the bill goes to the legislature for debate, and it may get merged with competing proposals. That means the 20% figure and the ICO framework are negotiating positions now, not law. I would expect the ownership cap to move first. Numbers like that usually do once the horse-trading starts.

Nobody outside the Korean legislature knows what the final text looks like. When it lands, the details will matter more than the headline.
