Binance CFO Kaiser Ng is leaving the exchange nine days after hackers drained more than $6 billion from it, the largest crypto theft on record. His replacement is Justin Yek, most recently vice president of finance at payments firm Airwallex, according to a statement from co-CEO Yi He reported by Bloomberg and Reuters.

## What Binance says

Yi He said Ng "has decided to pursue a new venture" after more than five years at the company, and that a transition plan had been implemented over the past six months. That timing detail is doing a lot of work. Six months ago is well before the Oct. 29 hack, which is the first thing anyone reading this announcement thinks about.

The statement left out two things: when Ng's departure takes effect, and what the new venture is. Ng has been CFO since January 2022 and previously worked at rival exchange Kraken, according to his LinkedIn profile.

## The hack, briefly

The numbers are still hard to get your head around. Attackers pulled more than $6 billion from Binance across multiple blockchains on Oct. 29. The FBI has attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group.

Binance had replenished about 95% of the affected assets with its own funds as of Nov. 2, when it completed a proof of reserves snapshot and began restoring withdrawals. The hackers, meanwhile, have swapped stolen tokens for $124 million in bitcoin, according to blockchain analytics firm Elliptic.

## My read

Companies do plan CFO successions, and five-plus years is a normal run for an executive before moving on. The six-month plan could be entirely real. Binance also has every incentive to say it was planned, and outsiders have no way to check.

Here is what I keep coming back to. If you were the CFO of a company that just lost $6 billion in a day, and you had a graceful exit already lined up, announcing it nine days later would still look bad. Delaying would look worse. "Planned all along" is what a company says in this spot whether it is true or not, so the denial itself carries almost no information.

The part that is not in dispute: Binance absorbed a $6 billion hit and covered about 95% of it from its own funds within days. That is a show of balance-sheet strength most exchanges could not match. It is also a reminder of how much risk concentrates inside one company. The largest theft in crypto history is nine days old, the stolen money is being laundered into bitcoin right now, and the person who has watched the books since January 2022 is on his way out.

Whether the timing is coincidence or consequence, nobody outside Binance knows. I doubt we ever find out.
