Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on November 23, 2026, after a brief ouster tied to multiple alleged affairs. The board announced the reversal just hours after former President Donald Trump called into a board meeting to push for Huang's return, according to Semafor reporting cited by TechCrunch【https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/elon-musk-and-others-rejoice-as-jensen-huang-is-reinstated-at-nvidia-amid-scandal/】.

The scandal began in early November when Huang admitted to cheating on his wife of 40 years. One of the alleged partners, Alice Huang, is an executive at Singapore‑based Megaspeed and is suspected by U.S. officials of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Megaspeed bought $4.8 billion of Nvidia chips in the past two years, and both the U.S. and Singapore have investigated the firm for possible illegal chip transfers to China, as reported by The New York Times and referenced in the timeline article【https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-affair-with-alleged-chinese-spy-a-timeline/】.

A second affair surfaced involving Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reports directly to Huang. Five Nvidia employees told the Times that Powell was also involved, and a Reuters report later added a third unnamed employee to the list. Huang's response to a Times reporter was terse: "I don't think you should be talking to me directly," he said before hanging up.

The board initially said it would not reinstate Huang and hired an executive search firm to find a permanent replacement. Interim CEO Colette Kress led the company through a strong earnings report on November 17, posting $115.8 billion in revenue, a 103 % year‑over‑year increase. Kress also announced that Nvidia would stop selling its H200 chips to China, effectively ending a controversial loophole in the U.S. export ban【https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-affair-with-alleged-chinese-spy-a-timeline/】.

Industry reaction was swift. Elon Musk praised the move on X, likening the earlier ouster to "kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you're on a winning streak." Sam Altman simply posted "Jensen!" while other tech leaders, CZ of Binance, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick, joined an open letter urging Huang's return. Hedge‑fund manager Bill Ackman called the board's initial decision "not acting in the best interest of shareholders," and noted that the stock rebound after the reinstatement validated his view【https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/elon-musk-and-others-rejoice-as-jensen-huang-is-reinstated-at-nvidia-amid-scandal/】.

Political scrutiny remains. Senator John Cornyn asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?" and Senator Pete Ricketts reminded the board that Huang still faces testimony before three House committees on December 1 and a public Senate Banking Committee hearing slated for early December. Trump's claim on Truth Social that "the United States government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return poses any risk to national security" has not quelled congressional concerns【https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/elon-musk-and-others-rejoice-as-jensen-huang-is-reinstated-at-nvidia-amid-scandal/】.

What does this mean for Nvidia's AI dominance? The company's chip business is a linchpin in the U.S.-China tech race. Removing the H200 from China may protect the firm from export penalties, but it also cedes a market segment to rivals. Huang's personal controversies could invite tighter oversight of Nvidia's supply chain, especially given the Megaspeed connection. The board's willingness to reverse its decision under political pressure suggests that corporate governance is being tested by both market forces and geopolitical stakes.

The next few weeks will likely reveal how much congressional testimony will affect Nvidia's operations. If lawmakers uncover concrete evidence of espionage, the company could face new restrictions that would ripple through the AI hardware ecosystem. Until then, investors and observers are left watching a drama that blends personal scandal, tech power, and national security, all at once.
