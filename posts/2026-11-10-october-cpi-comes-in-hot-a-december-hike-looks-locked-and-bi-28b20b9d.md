U.S. inflation came in hotter than forecast in October, and Bitcoin sold off a little harder on the news.

The Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October, per CoinDesk's writeup of the BLS release. That is down from 0.6% in September but above the 0.3% economists expected. Core CPI, which strips out food and energy, rose 0.3%, matching September and beating forecasts of 0.2%.

One oddity: there is no year-over-year CPI number this time. The BLS never published an October 2025 report because of the government shutdown back then, so there is nothing to compare against. One missing report from a year ago, and the annual number everyone quotes is just gone.

## Waller wants a hike, maybe a big one

The bigger story is what the Fed does with this. Fed Governor Chris Waller said overnight that the central bank should hike at its December meeting even if the CPI report came in soft, and he floated the idea that a 50-basis-point move could be appropriate if the number was strong. The number was strong.

Traders got the message. The 2-year Treasury yield jumped 14 basis points to 4.69%, and the 10-year added 6.5 basis points to 4.93%. CoinDesk says a December hike now "appears completely assured," with markets pricing a small chance the Fed goes for 50.

## Bitcoin takes the hit

Bitcoin was already down before the print and lost about another 1% after it, trading near $73,600. Stock futures fell too, the Nasdaq 100 off 1.15% and the S&P 500 down 0.5%.

This is the part of the Bitcoin story that never quite goes away. The asset that gets pitched as an inflation hedge keeps trading like a high-beta tech stock on rate days. Hot inflation means higher rates, higher rates mean less appetite for risk, and Bitcoin sits at the far end of the risk curve. Maybe the hedge thesis works on a decade timescale. On a CPI morning, it does not.

I keep coming back to the rate path. If the Fed hikes in December and Waller's 50-basis-point idea is even half serious, the easy-money tailwind behind this cycle gets a lot weaker. Nobody knows how this plays out, but a surprise 50 would be a real test of how much of Bitcoin's price is conviction and how much is liquidity.
