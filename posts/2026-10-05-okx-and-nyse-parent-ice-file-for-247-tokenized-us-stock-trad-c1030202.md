The parent company of the New York Stock Exchange wants to trade tokenized U.S. stocks around the clock, and it has told the SEC so.

OKXICE, the 50-50 joint venture between crypto exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), has notified the SEC of plans for a tokenized stock trading venue, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/05/okx-and-nyse-s-owner-file-for-round-the-clock-tokenized-trading-in-u-s-stocks). Co-chair Andrew Cuomo, the former New York governor, announced the move on X.

## What the venue would look like

The plan is to start with more than 60 companies listed on U.S. exchanges. A public notice dated Oct. 4 lists 63 proposed symbols, [per Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/okx-nyse-parent-file-to-launch-tokenized-us-stock-platform). Nvidia is on it, along with Apple and Microsoft. Tesla made the list too, as did crypto names like Strategy and Coinbase, plus Circle and Bitgo.

Trading would run 24 hours a day, seven days a week, through permissioned Uniswap v4 liquidity pools deployed on XLayer. Each tokenized stock would be paired against a stablecoin. USDC is one option, with USDG and USDT also listed. The shares are supposed to carry the same rights as regular stock. That includes dividends and voting.

Read that middle part again. The owner of the NYSE intends to route stock trades through Uniswap pools. Five years ago that sentence was gibberish. Now it is in an SEC filing.

## The rule that makes it possible

The filing leans on the SEC's "Innovation Exemption," issued Sept. 17, which lets qualifying venues trade tokenized National Market System stocks through automated market makers and liquidity pools. The exemption is temporary and runs five years.

It comes with guardrails. Companies whose shares get listed have 30 days to object to being tokenized. Launch is still subject to those regulatory steps, so the 63-symbol list is a proposal, not a done deal.

OKX and ICE formed the venture in June to build infrastructure for tokenized financial products. Cuomo framed the filing as a first step, telling CoinDesk that "tokenization is gathering real momentum" and that 24/7 onchain markets could make trading and settlement "more efficient, accessible and global."

## What I keep circling

My honest reaction is that this is impressive and kind of unsettling at the same time.

The impressive part is straightforward. CoinDesk notes that tokenized shares can trade outside normal market hours and settle faster than traditional ones. Those are real improvements, and ICE putting its name on them gives the idea a kind of institutional weight that crypto-native projects never managed on their own.

The unsettling part is liquidity. An automated market maker for Apple stock at 3 a.m. on a Sunday is only as good as the capital sitting in the pool. Neither report says how the underlying shares are custodied or who provides liquidity at launch. Neither says what happens to prices when the traditional market is closed and the pool is thin. Those are not details. They are the whole product.

Worth watching: the 30-day objection window. If a few big names on that list of 63 decide they do not want their shares trading onchain overnight, the launch lineup gets smaller fast.

The five-year clock matters too. The SEC has not rewritten market structure. It has opened a supervised experiment with an expiration date. Whether tokenized stocks become the default way to trade or a curiosity from 2026 depends on things nobody can see yet, starting with whether anyone actually wants to buy Tesla from a liquidity pool at midnight.
