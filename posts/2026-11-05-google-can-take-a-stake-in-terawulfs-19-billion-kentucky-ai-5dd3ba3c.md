Google has agreed to backstop Anthropic's 20-year, $19 billion lease for 401 megawatts of computing capacity at bitcoin miner TeraWulf's Kentucky data center project, and if Anthropic fails to pay its rent, Google can end up owning a piece of the project.

The arrangement was disclosed in a Wednesday TeraWulf filing and reported by CoinDesk. The point of the backstop is to help TeraWulf secure financing for the project, which is expected to begin delivering capacity in late 2027, with deliveries continuing through the second quarter of 2028.

## The default mechanics

If Anthropic defaults on rent or becomes insolvent, Google can step in as the tenant and take over the lease. Under certain conditions it can instead pay a termination fee and take an ownership stake in Raylan Data, the TeraWulf subsidiary operating the project. The stake is in the subsidiary, not in TeraWulf itself.

The math is proportional. The termination fee gets divided by the total cost of developing and building the facility, including land, infrastructure, equipment, and financing. A payment equal to 30% of total project cost would give Google a 30% stake.

There are limits. For the first six years after its support takes effect, Google generally has to take over the lease rather than terminate it. The exception is if the TPU pods installed in the halls, Google's own AI chips, can use less than half the power set aside for the tenant. And the obligations end if Anthropic, or a parent or subsidiary, holds an investment-grade credit rating for at least three months.

The agreements were signed on November 4.

## Read it as an option, not a favor

I keep coming back to what this structure says about Anthropic's balance sheet. A company signing $19 billion in 20-year lease commitments still needs Google to stand behind the rent before a bitcoin miner's lenders will finance the buildings. That is not a small tell. The investment-grade escape clause only matters if Anthropic lacks that rating today, or at least if TeraWulf's financiers refuse to assume it has one.

For Google, this looks cheap. It was already supplying the chips. The filing describes TPU pods installed in the halls, so the tenant's workloads at this site run on Google silicon either way. Now Google also holds what amounts to a call option on 401 megawatts of built-out capacity. If Anthropic stumbles, Google gets the lease or a discounted equity slice of the project. If Anthropic pays its bills for two decades, Google pays nothing.

For TeraWulf, the deal is more evidence that the most valuable thing a bitcoin miner owns is not its hashrate. It is the power and the real estate. Mining is what you do with a site while you wait for a better tenant, and right now AI labs are the better tenants, even ones whose rent needs a guarantor.

The risk chain is worth naming plainly. TeraWulf's financing rests on Anthropic's rent, which rests on Google's guarantee, which only gets called if Anthropic runs into trouble. Everyone in that chain is betting that demand for AI compute in 2027 and 2028 looks enough like demand today to keep the checks clearing. Nobody knows how that plays out. Google just structured its end so it wins either way.
