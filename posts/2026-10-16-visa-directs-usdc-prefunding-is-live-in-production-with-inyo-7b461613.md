Visa Direct's stablecoin prefunding service is now in commercial production, with payments processor Inyo Global as the first to offer it to clients. Visa announced the launch on Thursday, [The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-16-visa-direct-moves-usdc-prefunding-into-commercial-production-with-inyo-global-418802).

The mechanics: eligible businesses on Inyo's platform can fund cross-border payouts through Visa Direct in USDC, in near real time, including when traditional funding windows are closed. That last part is the whole point. Bank funding windows shut on weekends and holidays. USDC does not.

Inyo's customers are money transmitters, fintechs, banks, and payment companies, and its platform handles payouts through Visa Direct across more than 180 countries. The prefunding option runs through Inyo's existing APIs, subject to eligibility and geographic restrictions.

## A year of pilots before this

This launch did not come out of nowhere. Visa started testing stablecoin prefunding in September 2025, initially with USDC and EURC. That trial was about how businesses funded their payments, not what recipients received. In November it tested USDC payouts directly to eligible recipients. In August it named zerohash as the technology and regulatory infrastructure provider for stablecoin funding and payouts through Visa Direct.

Earlier this month, Visa settled $750,000 in payment obligations with Lloyds during a seven-day USDC trial. The funds reached Visa in less than an hour, including over a weekend, the firms said.

Inyo co-founder and Chief Product and Technology Officer Robson Dantas said the integration gives clients a funding option "available around the clock and runs through the same Inyo APIs they already use." Vira Platonova, global head of Visa Direct, put it more bluntly: "The next chapter for stablecoins is adoption."

## My take

This is the version of stablecoin adoption I find credible. No consumer app. No token launch. Just a card network using USDC as back-office funding plumbing so its clients can move money on a Saturday. The recipient on the other end still gets local currency. Most people touching these payouts will never know a stablecoin was involved.

That is also the bear case, sort of. If stablecoins win as invisible infrastructure, the winners are incumbents like Visa and Circle, not the open networks crypto people actually care about. Visa keeps its toll booth. It just accepts a new currency at the gate.

The caveats are real. This is one processor, with eligibility requirements and geographic restrictions Visa did not detail. Commercial production is not the same as volume. Whether meaningful money flows through this is a question nobody can answer yet, and Visa has a long history of crypto announcements that fade quietly.

Still, $750,000 settled with Lloyds in under an hour, over a weekend, is a concrete number from a real bank. That is more than most stablecoin press releases offer.
