# North Korea's TraderTraitor likely behind the $6.3 billion Binance hack

**Date:** 2026‑10‑30

---

Binance lost roughly $6.3 billion to hackers on Thursday, and the firms that trace stolen crypto for a living say North Korea is probably responsible.

**TRM Labs** attributed the attack to **TraderTraitor**, a sub‑unit of the Lazarus Group, with *high confidence*.  According to TRM, the attacker moved about **$6.36 billion** out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in roughly **20 minutes**.  That would make it the largest cryptocurrency theft on record, surpassing the **$1.46 billion** Bybit hack of February 2025, which the FBI also linked to North Korea.

---

## The evidence

The attribution is coming from several directions at once.  **ZachXBT** found that funds stolen from Binance and from the earlier Bitget exploit converged at the same address after moving through THORChain.  *"This indicates the same actor is likely behind both incidents,"* he said, and the Bitget attack had already been attributed to TraderTraitor.

**Chainalysis** CEO **Jonathan Levin** put the number slightly higher, at **$6.4 billion**, citing *"links to previous hack."*

**Blockaid** CEO **Ido Ben‑Natan** said the tactics and sophistication match DPRK operations, noting that DPRK‑linked actors have been tied to the **Kelp DAO**, **Bitget**, and **Drift** incidents this year.

A person familiar with crypto investigations told **CoinDesk** there are *"very few actors who could pull something like this off,"* but cautioned that the attribution is not definitive yet.  Lazarus crews are usually faster and more efficient at moving and selling stolen funds than Thursday's hackers were.

---

## What Binance is saying

**Binance** says it worked with blockchain partners to freeze roughly **$3 billion** in affected assets and has started recovering funds for users.  Deposits were unaffected; withdrawals are suspended and are expected to resume within a day or two.  The company also said it will cover all losses.

The breach itself is still murky.  Binance first suggested a compromised laptop, then said a follow‑up investigation uncovered additional *"points of breach."*  It also said it found *"no evidence of any wallet private key leak."*

**Kok Tin Gan**, CEO of cybersecurity firm **NyxLab**, offered a blunt read: *"This looks like one signature that gave the attacker a never‑expiring, full‑access token to a Binance hot wallet."*

---

## The pattern

The **TraderTraitor** playbook is well documented.  The FBI says the group targets company employees through social engineering.  In the **May 2024 DMM Bitcoin** theft (over **$300 million**), an attacker posing as a recruiter sent a malicious coding test to an employee of wallet provider **Ginco**, allowing the attacker to manipulate a legitimate transaction request.

**Changpeng Zhao**, who founded Binance in 2017, posted on X that *"User funds are safe!"* while acknowledging the incident *"does look like a hack on Binance."*  He added that, as a shareholder, he expects all user funds will be fully covered.

Two things stick with me.  The freeze is genuinely new.  In past mega‑hacks the money was gone before anyone finished the press release, and this time roughly half the haul got locked down.  The tracing and coordination plumbing works, at least sometimes.  But the entry point never changes - it is not some brilliant cryptographic break.  It is a compromised laptop, or a fake recruiter with a coding test.

South Korea's spy agency said back in 2022 that North Korea's ability to steal digital assets was among the best in the world, estimating **$1.1 billion** stolen over five years.

Whether the attribution holds, and whether the **$6.3 billion** in covered losses actually gets covered, are questions nobody can answer yet.
