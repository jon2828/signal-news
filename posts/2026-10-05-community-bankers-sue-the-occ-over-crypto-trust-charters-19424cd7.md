The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency in federal court on Friday, accusing the regulator of exceeding its legal authority by granting national trust bank charters to crypto firms, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/02/bank-group-sues-u-s-regulator-over-granting-crypto-trust-charters).

The complaint goes at the OCC's chartering power directly. The ICBA says the agency is claiming "sweeping new powers to charter national trust banks that are not authorized by the National Bank Act," and that it is letting crypto companies into the U.S. banking system without the regulatory oversight community banks live under. The result, the group argues, puts small banks "at a severe competitive disadvantage."

## The same fight, a new venue

This is not the ICBA's first move this year. The group, one of the largest banking advocacy organizations in the U.S., typically representing smaller institutions, played a significant role in pushing back against the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, which failed to advance in the Senate last month. Per CoinDesk, the bill stalled in part because the bankers objected that its stablecoin provisions didn't protect them from direct competition with the deposit accounts that are core to their businesses.

Having won that round, the group is now going after the other door: the trust charters crypto firms have been pursuing as an entry point into U.S. banking and payments.

## My read

Two things are true at once here. The legal question is genuine. Whether the National Bank Act actually authorizes the OCC to charter these trust banks is a real fight, and a court will now have to answer it.

But let's not pretend this is purely about statutory interpretation. The ICBA's members hold deposits. Crypto firms with trust charters and stablecoin products threaten those deposits. The fairness argument, hold them to our standards, is also a moat argument. It was one during the CLARITY Act fight and it is one now.

I keep coming back to the timing. The Senate just showed it cannot move a market structure bill, partly because of banking industry pressure. If the courts close the charter route too, crypto firms are out of doors to knock on. Maybe that is the point.

Nobody knows how the lawsuit lands. What seems safe to say is that the charter question will not be settled quietly by regulators anymore. It will be settled in court, with the banking industry paying the lawyers.
