SpaceX and Tesla are exploring a merger, Elon Musk said Tuesday, and if the deal happens the combined company would hold 30,221 bitcoin.

Musk confirmed the talks on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call. Both companies have formed special committees of independent directors to assess a tie-up, and he stressed that the committees have not agreed on the terms of any transaction.

## The bitcoin math

The combined treasury breaks down like this: SpaceX holds 18,712 BTC and Tesla holds 11,509 BTC. Add them and you get 30,221 BTC, which is the number that turned this into a crypto story at all.

I'll be honest, the bitcoin angle feels like the least interesting part of the deal. A merger doesn't change what the coins do. It changes whose name is on the account. But "30,000 bitcoin under one roof" is the kind of headline that travels, and here we are.

## The kabuki dance

Musk owns about 36% of SpaceX outright, rising to 48.4% when you count restricted shares and unexercised options, according to a June filing. His Tesla stake is 19.8%. Because he sits on both sides of the table, he is barred from the committee discussions.

His description of the arrangement is better than anything I could write: "It's an odd kabuki dance where two groups of people are talking, and I can't be in the conversation."

My problem is the other thing he said. "Do I think it would be a good idea, yes," he told listeners, before adding that shareholders have to be supportive. When the CEO of both companies, a man holding 48.4% of one and 19.8% of the other, announces on an earnings call that he wants the deal, the independent committees are not really deciding anything. They are pricing the paperwork. The kabuki line is funny because it is true, but I don't think Musk meant the whole process is theater. I think it is anyway.

## The earnings underneath

The merger talk landed on top of a strong quarter for SpaceX. Revenue came in at $14.58 billion against a $12.7 billion consensus, and earnings of 35 cents a share more than doubled the 15-cent forecast. Shares jumped about 9% to $161 in Wednesday premarket trading.

The number I keep coming back to is capital expenditure: $19.59 billion for the quarter, of which $16.44 billion went to AI. That is an enormous bet, and it explains why CoinDesk describes SpaceX as "space and AI-focused" rather than just a rocket company. The results also helped revive battered AI stocks, per the report.

Tesla reports after Wednesday's close, with analysts forecasting revenue of $28.13 billion and adjusted earnings of 44 cents a share. If Tesla misses while SpaceX rides a beat, the merger math gets harder, not easier. A weak print makes Tesla the junior partner in a deal its own CEO is pitching from the other side.

Nobody outside those two committees knows what terms would look like, and by Musk's own account there are no terms yet. Tonight's Tesla numbers will say more about where this goes than any committee statement.
