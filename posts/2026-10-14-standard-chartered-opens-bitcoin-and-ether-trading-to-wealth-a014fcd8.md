# Standard Chartered opens Bitcoin and Ether trading to wealthy UAE clients

*Date: 2026-10-14*

Standard Chartered is now letting wealthy clients in the United Arab Emirates buy and sell Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) directly through their existing bank accounts - a service described by **CoinDesk** as one of the first of its kind from an international bank in the UAE [1].

The service is limited to professional investors. **CoinTelegraph** reports that eligible individuals must have at least 4 million AED (about $1.1 million) in net assets, excluding their primary residence, and must demonstrate sufficient investment knowledge and experience or be represented by a licensed entity [2].

---

## How it works

The bank handles custody and settlement in-house through what it calls a **closed-loop model**: clients never manage a crypto wallet or interact with a third-party exchange. Trading is executed on **Zodia Markets**, a venue backed by Standard Chartered's SC Ventures unit, and the whole operation operates under a licence from Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) [1].

This is the first time Standard Chartered is offering crypto trading directly to wealthy retail clients; the bank has previously only provided institutional spot-trading services. It launched institutional Bitcoin and Ether spot trading through its UK arm in **July 2025**, and expanded that institutional service to the UAE in early September 2024 [2].

> "Digital assets are moving into mainstream wealth conversations, and our clients are increasingly looking for the security and confidence that comes from investing through a regulated and trusted institution," said **Samir Subberwal**, global head of wealth solutions for retail products and data analytics at Standard Chartered [2].

## My take

I keep coming back to the closed-loop part. On one hand, this is real: a major international bank, a regulatory licence, Bitcoin and Ether sitting next to a client's deposit accounts. Five years ago that sentence would have read like parody.

On the other hand, the design strips out the thing that makes Bitcoin, Bitcoin. No wallet means the coins stay inside the bank's ecosystem, at least as the announcement describes it. You get price exposure, the bank keeps the keys, collects the fees, and owns the relationship. What's being sold here is Bitcoin as a line-item on a statement, not Bitcoin as an exit from the banking system.

That may be exactly what these clients want. If you have $1.1 million in net assets and a private banker, you probably don't want to manage seed phrases. Fair enough. But the gap between "bank sells you Bitcoin exposure" and "you own Bitcoin" is worth naming, because the marketing will not name it.

The wider UAE picture matters too. **Kraken's** parent company **Payward** received VARA approval on a Monday, and neobank **Revolut** secured in-principle approval in July, both reported by **CoinTelegraph** [2]. Dubai is issuing licences at a steady clip, and banks and exchanges are racing to become the default on-ramp for wealthy Gulf money. Standard Chartered is the first international bank to roll out a direct retail crypto product in the region.

Whether closed-loop crypto counts as adoption or as an expensive wrapper, I can't say cleanly. Probably both, depending on who's buying.
