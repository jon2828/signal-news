# Standard Chartered and SBI back crypto insurer Soter in $66 million Series B

*Date: 2026‑11‑05*

Standard Chartered's venture arm and Japan's SBI Group have invested in crypto insurer **Soter Insure**, joining a round that takes the company's total backing to **$23 million plus 500 BTC and 2,000 ETH**【source】. At current prices the crypto portion is worth about **$43 million**, putting Soter's Series B at roughly **$66 million**【source】.

## What Soter sells

Soter writes policies against losses from hacks, theft, and software failures. Customers can fund policies in bitcoin or ether, and claims are paid out in the same currency rather than dollars.

## Funding details

The round was led by **Galaxy**, with earlier investors including **Coinbase Ventures, Franklin Templeton, and Brevan Howard Digital**【source】. SC Ventures and SBI joined the Galaxy‑led round, bringing the total backing to the figures above.

## The balance sheet is the story

Soter founder and CEO **Henson Orser** told CoinDesk:

> "Any BTC or ETH we have raised along the way has been used to fund insurance programs denominated in that native currency and not as working capital in the general operating account."

He added that the crypto raised is used **solely** to fund insurance programs, not as general operating capital.

I keep coming back to that line. If your liabilities are denominated in bitcoin, holding dollars against them is a mismatch. When bitcoin rallies, your claims become more expensive in fiat terms while your reserves sit still. Holding the asset you insure fixes that - it's the same matching logic any insurer uses, applied to a far more volatile asset.

The flip side is harder to wave away. An insurer sitting on 500‑plus BTC is itself a custody target, which is a strange position for a company selling hack insurance. Claims in this business tend to cluster; hacks and software failures often arrive in waves during market stress, exactly when an insurer least wants to pay out. Matching reserves to liabilities in native units handles price risk but does nothing for correlation risk.

## Why banks are buying in

The demand story is straightforward: banks and asset managers are increasing their crypto exposure and want protection against hacks and theft. What's newer is the shape of the bet. Standard Chartered and SBI are taking equity in the insurer itself rather than simply buying policies, joining names like Franklin Templeton and Brevan Howard that got in earlier.

That reads to me like institutions deciding insurance is the bottleneck for their own crypto plans, then paying to widen it. Whether a crypto‑denominated insurer can actually survive a real claims wave is a different question, and nobody has answered it yet.
