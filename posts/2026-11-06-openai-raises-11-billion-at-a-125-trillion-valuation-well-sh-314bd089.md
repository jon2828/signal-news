OpenAI has raised $11 billion so far in a new funding round that values the company at $1.25 trillion, according to a Bloomberg report covered by TechCrunch on Friday.

## The ask and the close

The round isn't finished. Bloomberg's sources say it's expected to close in the coming weeks and could total about $20 billion. In late September, the same outlet reported OpenAI was looking to raise $30 billion in this round. The current figures fall well short of that target.

I keep coming back to that gap. A $1.25 trillion valuation sounds like a triumph, and the headline number will get treated as one. But a company that went out looking for $30 billion and lands closer to $20 billion is telling you something about appetite. The checks are still coming. They're smaller than the ask.

TechCrunch says it reached out to OpenAI for comment and hadn't heard back at publication.

## Anthropic is hearing the same thing

Hours before the OpenAI news, Bloomberg reported that potential buyers of shares in Anthropic's IPO are eyeing a substantially lower valuation than the company had targeted. Some potential buyers believe Anthropic could be valued as low as $1.1 trillion, compared with the $2 trillion investors were said to be expecting roughly a month ago.

One company missing its number is a company problem. Two of the biggest AI labs missing their numbers in the same week is a market signal.

## The politics under all of this

TechCrunch ties the timing to the Democratic landslide in the U.S. midterms and what it describes as overwhelmingly negative voter sentiment around data centers. The result, per the report, is jitters throughout the market and expectations of regulatory challenges ahead.

This is the part I'd watch. Valuations can absorb a lot of hype correction. They're worse at absorbing policy risk. If data centers have become something voters are actively angry about, and the market now expects regulators to respond, then building AI infrastructure just picked up a political cost it didn't have a year ago. That doesn't stop the buildout. It does make trillion-dollar price tags harder to defend.

## The IPO clock

OpenAI had previously intimated it would go public this year, then delayed. The expectation now is an IPO sometime next year. Which means this round, whenever it closes, is likely the last private pricing before public markets get a vote. Public markets tend to be less sentimental than private ones.

Maybe the round closes at the full $20 billion and everyone moves on. Maybe Anthropic prices fine and this all looks like noise in six months. Or maybe this is what the early edge of a repricing looks like. Nobody knows how this plays out, and anyone who claims they do is selling something.
