# Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to oust Jensen Huang

**Date:** 2026-11-11

Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to oust Jensen Huang, the co‑founder and CEO who has led the company for 33 years. The Wall Street Journal reports the board is meeting **as soon as Wednesday** to determine Huang's future, amid reports of a personal scandal that unfolded over the past few days.

On Sunday, Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman purported by U.S. intelligence to be a Chinese spy. On Wednesday evening, the New York Times reported that Huang also had an affair with an executive who reports to him. Nvidia stock fell about **8 %** in after‑hours trading on Wednesday and is down roughly **11 %** over the past five days.

---

## The part that matters

The affair is the headline; the intelligence angle is the story.

Nvidia designs the chips that power the AI boom, making it one of the most valuable companies in the world. The CEO's admission of a relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence believes is a Chinese spy adds a national‑security dimension to the personal scandal. While the exact impact on the company is still uncertain, boards typically act when they perceive a risk the company cannot absorb, not merely because of gossip.

The second reported affair, an involvement with an executive who reports to Huang, raises a clear governance concern. At a company where Huang has said he delegates very little and has about **60 direct reports**, such a relationship could be viewed as a significant leadership failure.

---

## The succession problem

The **60‑direct‑reports** detail is noteworthy. Huang built Nvidia around himself, and the company has no obvious successor or formal succession plan. Bloomberg reports that Nvidia is now working with an outside firm to identify candidates who could replace him. In other words, contingency planning is happening publicly and in real time.

For years the "all‑Huang" structure was praised as a strength, one decision‑maker, fast moves. That same structure means there is no clear "number two" who could step in immediately. Whoever the outside firm identifies will inherit a company where roughly 60 people were accustomed to reporting directly to one person, making the handoff especially challenging amid a scandal.

---

## The Musk side‑show

Elon Musk posted on X late Wednesday that hiring Huang "would be an honor." It's a low‑cost way for Musk to generate engagement, but the comment can be read as a signal that Huang would be a highly sought‑after talent if he were to leave Nvidia, rather than a literal job offer.

---

## What happens next?

All of the above comes from the Wall Street Journal, the New York Times, Bloomberg, and the TechCrunch recap. Nothing is a done deal yet. The board meeting is slated "as soon as Wednesday," so it may have already occurred or could be delayed.

Key things to watch:
- Any official comment from Nvidia confirming or denying the reports.
- How the stock reacts in the coming days.
- Whether the outside firm identifies a successor and how quickly that name is announced.

Nobody knows exactly how this will play out, but the combination of personal scandal, governance concerns, and a lack of a clear successor makes the situation unusually volatile for a company of Nvidia's size.
