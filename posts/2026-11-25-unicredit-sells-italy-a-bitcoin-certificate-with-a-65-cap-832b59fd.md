UniCredit, Italy's second-largest bank, has launched what it calls the country's first bitcoin-linked investment certificate for retail customers, a six-year product that tracks BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin ETP, caps returns at 65%, and promises to hand your money back at maturity even if bitcoin crashes. CoinDesk has the details.

## The terms

Minimum investment is 1,000 euros, placed through a UniCredit financial adviser. Subscriptions close Dec. 18, and the certificate matures on Dec. 22, 2032. If bitcoin rises, you collect the gains up to that 65% ceiling. A 1,000-euro stake tops out at 1,650 euros no matter how high bitcoin goes. If bitcoin falls, UniCredit says it will repay the initial investment in full at maturity.

You never own bitcoin. You own a certificate whose value depends on the performance of BlackRock's ETP. In plain terms, you own a promise from a bank, denominated in euros, that references a fund.

## The catches

Two of these are in the fine print. The third is mine.

First, the cap. Sixty-five percent over six years works out to roughly 8.7% a year, by my math, and only if bitcoin performs well enough to hit the ceiling. Anyone holding actual bitcoin keeps all of the upside. Certificate buyers hand everything above the ceiling to the bank.

Second, the protection only works if you hold to 2032. Sell early and you can lose money. And the guarantee itself is only as good as UniCredit's solvency. The bank says so itself: repayment depends on its ability to pay.

Third, the part nobody puts in the brochure. Getting your nominal euros back in 2032 is not the same as getting your purchasing power back. Six years of inflation makes a capital-protection promise softer than it sounds, even when the bank keeps it.

## Why banks like this shape

Structured products let banks sell crypto exposure to cautious clients without asking anyone to open a wallet or touch an exchange. The wrapper uses embedded options to trade upside for downside protection, a familiar fixed-income trick. The global structured products market exceeds $2 trillion in outstanding notional value, according to analysis from Numerix cited by CoinDesk. Bitcoin structured products are a tiny slice of that, and a fraction of the roughly $72 billion held in U.S.-listed spot bitcoin ETFs.

UniCredit reaches roughly 8 million retail customers in Italy, so even a niche product gets a big funnel.

## My read

I keep coming back to the irony. Bitcoin was built so you would not need a bank's promise. Now one of Europe's biggest banks is selling exposure to it as exactly that: a promise, with the upside trimmed and a footnote saying the promise depends on the bank staying solvent.

For a saver who would never touch an exchange, maybe that trade is worth making. But call it what it is: a bond with a bitcoin-flavored coupon, not bitcoin. The people who want actual bitcoin will keep buying actual bitcoin. The people who buy this will spend six years hoping the bank outlives the product.
