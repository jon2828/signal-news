Jensen Huang has resigned as NVIDIA's president and CEO and given up his board seat, effective immediately, [the company announced Thursday](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition). Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, becomes interim CEO and keeps the CFO job while she does it. Harvey C. Jones becomes the first chair of the board NVIDIA has ever had.

That is the full extent of what we know. The release runs several hundred words. Not one of them explains why Huang left.

## What the release says

Kress has been NVIDIA's CFO since joining the company in September 2013, after nearly 25 years in finance roles at companies including Cisco and Microsoft. The board says it is opening a search for a permanent CEO and will consider internal and external candidates. Jones, a technology executive and venture investor of more than 30 years who previously served as CEO and executive chairman of Synopsys, said "the Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team."

Kress's statement is the only place the release gestures at the scale of the departure. She promises to build on "the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years." The release itself calls NVIDIA "one of the most consequential technology companies in the world." Beyond that, the farewell amounts to a brief thank-you from the board.

## What the release doesn't say

Planned successions have a shape. A successor gets named in advance, the outgoing CEO sticks around for a handoff, and the departing founder usually gets a quote of his own. This has none of that. Huang is gone the same day, the interim is holding two jobs at once, and Huang himself is quoted nowhere in the announcement. I keep coming back to "effective immediately." Boards use those words when the decision is already done being debated.

The governance detail deserves a second look. NVIDIA has never had a chair of the board. The role was created the day the founder left, and it now sits above an interim CEO who also still runs finance. Whatever prompted the resignation, the board wanted oversight that is not management, and it wanted that oversight in place right away.

Then there is the timing. NVIDIA reports third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, five days after this announcement, and the release makes a point of saying the report comes "as previously scheduled." That phrase is doing work. You include it when you are worried readers will assume the schedule is the least of what is changing.

## What happens next

Nobody outside that boardroom knows why Huang resigned, and the release is not telling. Tuesday's earnings call, set for 2 p.m. PT after results at roughly 1:20 p.m. PT, is the first chance to hear Kress address it. Expect careful wording and a lot of talk about continuity. A 33-year run that ends in a same-day press release gets explained eventually, one way or another.
