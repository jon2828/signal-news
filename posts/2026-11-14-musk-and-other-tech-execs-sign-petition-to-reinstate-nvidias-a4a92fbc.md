More than 115,000 people have signed a petition calling on Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang, the CEO who resigned Thursday after confessing to an affair with a chip executive suspected by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese spy. TechCrunch reports the count as of Saturday afternoon, citing the petition's own website.

The signer breakdown is the interesting part. According to the site, the petition has 5,965 current Nvidia employees, 1,108 former employees, and 63,063 shareholders. That leaves roughly 45,000 signers who are none of the above, just people on the internet with an opinion. The report doesn't say how the site verifies shareholder status, or anything else. Named supporters include Elon Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan.

## What the petition asks for

The petition says its signers are "concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process" and demands the board "bring him back while an independent investigation establishes the facts." It also calls for a fair investigation and more information released to the public. The line doing the work: "Jensen deserves due process, not mob justice."

I keep tripping over one word in there: removed. Every account says Huang resigned. He confessed, stepped down four days later, and agreed to testify before Congress. TechCrunch also notes that news reports have suggested Huang engaged in multiple extramarital affairs. Resigning after a confession is not mob justice. It is the ordinary sequence of events when a CEO admits to a relationship with someone U.S. intelligence treats as a spy risk. Due process is a shield for people contesting the facts. Huang, so far, is not contesting them.

## Musk's signature

Musk lending his name to a due process petition is on brand. Public campaigns cost him nothing and keep him at the center of whatever story is moving through tech that week. His signature is the reason this petition is news at all, and I suspect everyone involved knows that.

## The part that matters

The petition will not bring Huang back. Petitions do not reinstate CEOs. Boards do, and Nvidia's board has already named CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent replacement.

The actual stakes sit at the bottom of the TechCrunch report. Nvidia's stock fell sharply through the week, and Huang's departure is expected to leave a big gap in AI industry funding. Read that second point again. When one man's exit is expected to blow a hole in funding across an entire sector, that tells you how much of the AI boom ran through a single person's judgment. That person is now waiting to testify before Congress.

Nobody knows how the testimony goes or who ends up running Nvidia. The petition is noise. The funding gap is the signal.
