Moody's Ratings and Chainlink have completed a pilot that distributes credit ratings across multiple blockchain networks at once, using Chainlink's Cross-Chain Interoperability Protocol (CCIP), [The Block reports](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-11-10-moodys-chainlink-ccip-cross-chain-credit-ratings-pilot-437245).

## What actually happened

The testnet pilot used Moody's Token Integration Engine to push credit insights to permissioned and permissionless networks simultaneously. The pitch: a tokenized asset on one chain and a fund on another could both read the same Moody's data, while Moody's keeps its existing publication and governance processes intact.

This builds on Moody's earlier move onchain. Earlier this year the agency launched its token engine with an initial deployment on the Canton Network. The CCIP pilot takes that from one chain to many at once.

Fabian Astic, Moody's Global Head of Digital Economy, framed it as a bet on fragmentation. "The future of tokenized finance is unlikely to reside on a single blockchain, platform, or market infrastructure," he said, adding that investors will expect ratings access regardless of the technology underneath an asset.

## The fine print

Here is the part I would underline if press releases came with a red pen. The release itself says this was a testnet demonstration using representative data. No actual ratings were published onchain. No live dissemination processes were involved.

This is a plumbing test. It shows Moody's data can move across chains. It says nothing about whether anyone needs it to yet.

## The demand question

I keep coming back to one number in the announcement. Between January 2017 and October 2026, Moody's rated or assessed more than 70 digital issuances, including digital bonds and tokenized funds. That works out to about seven a year over nearly a decade. The multi-chain distribution machinery is being built for a market that so far produces a trickle of rated product.

To be fair, Moody's has been active in crypto lately. It assigned [investment-grade ratings to Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin](https://www.theblock.co/news/web3/2026-10-28-moodys-ripple-rlusd-stablecoin-431969) in late October and gave [Sky Protocol a B3 rating](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-07-moodys-sky-protocol-b3-rating-first-stablecoin-protocol-417931) earlier that month. And Chainlink recently expanded its collaboration with S&P Global Ratings to bring vault risk assessments onchain, which makes CCIP look like a bid to become the default distribution rail for the ratings business, not just for Moody's.

That is a sensible position for both companies if tokenized finance ever reaches the volume the pitch decks promise. But a demo with stand-in data is a demo with stand-in data. The version that matters, real ratings on live chains, has not happened yet.
