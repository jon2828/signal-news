# Standard Chartered plans institutional crypto and tokenized‑asset custody in Singapore

*Date: 2026‑10‑08*

Standard Chartered said on Thursday that it plans to offer digital‑asset custody services in Singapore for selected cryptocurrencies and stablecoins, as well as tokenized real‑world assets. The service will be aimed at institutional clients and corporate clients that qualify as accredited investors. The announcement includes the usual caveats: it is subject to regulatory requirements, no start date has been set, and no list of supported assets has been provided. When Cointelegraph asked which cryptocurrencies would be supported, the bank did not respond.

The service will sit inside Standard Chartered's financing and securities‑services business rather than being offered as a retail product.

---

## What Standard Chartered has been assembling

This is not a first‑time foray. In May, the bank announced it would acquire Zodia Custody's custody business and spin out Zodia Solutions after shareholders approved the deal. Last month it began offering institutional spot Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) trading through its Dubai‑based foreign‑exchange platform. In June, it signed a banking agreement with CoinMENA that supports the exchange's fiat on‑ and off‑ramps, client‑money accounts and virtual‑account transaction management. Its digital‑asset custody business already operates in the United Arab Emirates, and it also serves clients in Luxembourg and Hong Kong.

Singapore fills an obvious gap on that map. The bank described the city‑state as a "leading financial and innovation hub" and framed the launch as part of its global strategy.

> "As adoption grows, we are seeing institutional use cases and client interest emerge around tokenised funds, ETFs and precious metals, as clients look for more efficient ways to hold, transfer and mobilise assets," said Ying Ying Tan, global head of digital assets and securities services, in an email to CoinDesk.  
> "Our Singapore launch further strengthens our ability to help clients bridge traditional and digital markets through secure, regulated and bank‑grade infrastructure."

---

## The fine print

No specific assets have been named, and no timeline has been provided. Regulatory approval is still pending, so the announcement remains an expression of intent.

Standard Chartered is not the first global bank to move in this direction. BNY Mellon recently expanded its digital‑asset custody platform to include USDC custody and minting, and later added staking, highlighting a broader trend among large custodians.

---

## My read

Custody is the segment of crypto that banks can embrace with relatively low regulatory friction. By charging institutions for safekeeping and related infrastructure, banks can generate fee income within a compliance framework, regardless of Bitcoin's price movements. Standard Chartered has been methodically acquiring and building the necessary components to support this business.

The inclusion of stablecoins and tokenized real‑world assets alongside traditional cryptocurrencies is notable. While many token‑ization announcements omit a launch date, this one follows that pattern. The bank likely sees genuine client demand and also wants to be positioned should demand materialise.

Whether the service becomes an operating business will depend on details the bank has not yet disclosed: the specific assets it will support and the start date, both contingent on regulatory approval.
