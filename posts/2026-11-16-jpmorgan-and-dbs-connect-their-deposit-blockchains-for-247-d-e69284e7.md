JPMorgan and DBS have linked their blockchain-based deposit networks, letting approved clients move U.S. dollars between Singapore and the U.S. at any hour, weekends included, [CoinDesk reported Monday](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/16/j-p-morgan-and-dbs-let-clients-move-dollars-across-borders-on-weekends).

## What they actually built

The connection ties DBS Token Services to Kinexys, JPMorgan's blockchain payments business. Settlement runs through Partior, a blockchain settlement network both banks helped found, and the whole thing feeds into Project BLOOM, a Monetary Authority of Singapore effort to make tokenized money safe and widely usable. DBS and JPMorgan co-lead the project's workgroup on getting different digital money systems to work with each other.

What moves across the link is tokenized deposits. The bank records a client's dollar balance as a token on its own chain, in addition to traditional rails. It is still a bank deposit. It is not a new cryptocurrency. The only change is that the balance can move at any hour.

This builds on a framework the two banks announced in November 2025, so it has been cooking for a year. And it has at least one real customer lined up: Ant International plans to use the link to manage cash across its businesses in Singapore and the U.S.

## The fine print

Two details are worth pulling out.

First, these are permissioned blockchains. Only approved banks and clients can use them. CoinDesk draws the contrast directly: unlike Bitcoin and Ethereum, where anyone can plug in and move value, this is a gated system. You need an account at one of two banks, plus their blessing, to participate.

Second, the "24/7" claim carries an asterisk. Transfers inside the blockchain networks run continuously, but moving money between traditional accounts and JPMorgan's blockchain accounts shuts down for a three-hour maintenance window every Saturday. Even the future of money needs a nap, apparently.

## What I make of it

I keep coming back to the irony. The problem this solves, that cross-border dollars used to sit still over the weekend, is entirely self-inflicted. Open networks already let anyone plug in and move value, as CoinDesk's own comparison notes. The banks looked at that model, decided the always-on part was worth copying, and rebuilt it inside a walled garden where they control the guest list.

To be fair, this looks like real infrastructure rather than a press-release pilot. Ant International is a genuine user with genuine cash to move, and the MAS involvement gives it regulatory weight. Tokenized deposits that actually settle between two major banks, across borders, on weekends, is more than most bank-blockchain announcements ever deliver.

But be clear about what this is not. It is not open. It is not permissionless. It is the existing correspondent banking system with better hours and a shared database. If you are an approved client, your weekends just got more productive. If you are anyone else, nothing changed.

Whether these permissioned networks ever connect to something the public can actually touch, nobody knows. The banks have no obvious reason to open the gates.
