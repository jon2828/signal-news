Congress is preparing to subpoena Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang over his affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Bloomberg reports that House Republicans are readying the subpoena, and House China committee chairman John Moolenaar said Wednesday on Fox Business that Huang has until the end of the day to agree to a closed-door briefing. If he refuses, he will likely be subpoenaed. Brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said his committee is already preparing one.

## How we got here

The Financial Times reported Friday on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer. US intelligence officials believe she was recruited by Chinese intelligence to gather information about the CEO and to influence Nvidia's campaign to loosen chip export restrictions. Huang admitted the affair and apologized in a company memo on Sunday. Nvidia's independent directors are investigating with outside legal counsel. Nvidia has not responded to requests for comment.

A week before the subpoena talk, Republicans Mast, Moolenaar, and Rick Crawford sent Huang a letter asking him to appear before their committees about the relationship.

## The worst possible story for Huang's China argument

Here is why this matters beyond the tabloid angle. Huang has spent years arguing that the US should keep selling AI chips to China. He called export controls a "failure" at a conference last year. In May he said it was "not wise" for the US and China to build two separate AI ecosystems. He met repeatedly with President Trump and administration officials last year, and afterward the government reversed restrictions on Nvidia's H20 chips and cleared exports of the more powerful H200.

The money explains the lobbying. Nvidia booked $7.9 billion in revenue from customers headquartered in China in the three months ended July 26, roughly 8% of its total. Reuters reported last week that about 600,000 H200 chips are sitting in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to the mainland, while Beijing pushes Chinese AI companies to buy domestic chips instead.

## My read

The spy-seduction framing oversells it. Huang's lobbying for China access predates his reported meeting with Alice Huang at an Nvidia event in early 2024. He is a salesman chasing one of the biggest AI markets on earth. He did not need a handler to push for H200 exports. The revenue line did that on its own.

But I keep coming back to the counterintelligence problem, because it has no clean resolution. If intelligence officials are right that she was recruited to gather information on him, the question becomes what he said in private. Pointing out that he held these pro-China views anyway is not a defense that satisfies a closed-door briefing. Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly warned officials close to Trump about the relationship earlier this year, which means the suspicion was circulating at the top of the administration long before the FT story broke.

Even if Nvidia's internal investigation clears Huang of sharing anything sensitive, the taint lingers. Every assurance Nvidia offers that it can keep advanced chips away from unauthorized customers now runs through a story about the CEO's own judgment. Whether Huang can convince Washington that Nvidia can still responsibly do business with China is an open question. Nobody knows how this plays out.
