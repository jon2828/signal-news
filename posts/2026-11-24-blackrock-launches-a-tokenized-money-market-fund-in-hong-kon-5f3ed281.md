BlackRock has launched its first tokenized money market fund in the Asia‑Pacific region, and it runs on stablecoins. The BlackRock HKD Digital Liquidity Fund opened with roughly $100 million in assets, according to a South China Morning Post report cited by Cointelegraph, with Standard Chartered and Ant International as seed investors.

## How it works

Professional investors in Hong Kong can subscribe and redeem shares using **HKDAP**, a Hong Kong‑dollar‑pegged stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial. Anchorpoint is a joint venture between Standard Chartered, telecom company **HKT**, and blockchain investor **Animoca Brands**.

Standard Chartered is everywhere in this deal. It is a seed investor, a co‑owner of the stablecoin issuer, and the fund's custodian, administrator, trustee, and tokenization agent - one bank filling six roles, which concentrates a lot of trust in a single institution.

The fund itself is deliberately dull. It began operations on **November 16** and invests in short‑term securities issued or guaranteed by governments, plus high‑grade bank deposits. The net seven‑day yield was **2.03 %** as of Tuesday. Nobody is getting rich off that; the point is the plumbing: institutions can now park stablecoin holdings in a yield‑bearing fund without converting back to fiat first.

Retail investors are locked out of the stablecoin side. They can only subscribe with conventional Hong Kong dollars, though the fund may also accept tokenized deposits.

## The context

This is the second tokenized money‑market‑fund announcement out of Hong Kong in a week. **HSBC** said on **November 19** that retail customers could invest in a tokenized fund using its own HKD‑backed stablecoin, **RedCoin (RCHKD)**. HSBC went retail where BlackRock went institutional.

Both moves sit on top of Hong Kong's stablecoin licensing regime. HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial received the city's first two issuer licences in April, eight months after the regulatory framework took effect. Hong Kong has clearly decided it wants to be the place where tokenized finance gets tested with real money, and the incumbents are obliging.

One detail worth noting: the Hong Kong dollar has been pegged to the US dollar since **1983**. A HKD stablecoin is, in effect, a dollar proxy in a local regulatory wrapper. My read is that this is part of the appeal - dollar exposure with Hong Kong rules.

Ant International's involvement is the other thing I keep coming back to. The company says its payment services support more than **300 payment methods across 220 markets** and process **over 20 million transactions daily**, and that integrating the fund would support round‑the‑clock liquidity management across its global operations. If that integration happens, the fund becomes infrastructure rather than a product.

## My take

This is the boring version of crypto adoption, and I mean that as a compliment. No memecoins, no leverage. Just a money‑market fund with a stablecoin rail bolted on, yielding **2.03 %**.

But watch the pattern. BlackRock, HSBC, Standard Chartered, and Ant are all building on Hong Kong's licensing regime within months of it going live. The race to tokenize the dull parts of finance (settlement, cash management, liquidity) is further along than most people tracking Bitcoin's price would guess. Whether that race produces open infrastructure or a set of walled gardens run by the same banks as before is the part nobody knows yet.
