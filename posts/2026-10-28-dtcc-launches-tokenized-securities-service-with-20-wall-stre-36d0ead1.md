The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation went live with its tokenization service on Wednesday, and 20 Wall Street firms, including JPMorgan, Citi, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America and UBS, have signed agreements to use it, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/28/wall-street-backbone-dtcc-brings-usd30-trillion-securities-closer-to-blockchain-with-major-banks).

## What launched

The service runs on two networks: Canton Network and DTCC's private Besu blockchain. At launch it supports select large-cap stocks from the Russell 1000 index, exchange-traded funds, and U.S. Treasury bills, notes and bonds. A DTCC spokesperson told CoinDesk that 34 securities are eligible at launch, representing roughly $30 trillion in market value.

The tokens are backed one-for-one by securities held at the Depository Trust Company, a DTCC subsidiary. They can move between registered wallets around the clock, with existing shareholder protections intact. Onchain settlement and collateral tools are planned from here.

## Why this one is different

I have read a lot of tokenization announcements over the years. Most are pilots that quietly die. This one deserves more attention because of who is running it.

DTCC is the plumbing of U.S. markets. Through its subsidiaries, it handles clearing, settlement and custody for much of the country's stock and bond trading, with more than $114 trillion in securities held at its depository. When a firm that central to market infrastructure puts securities on blockchain rails, tokenization stops being a sideshow.

The bank list matters too. Twenty firms signed on at launch suggests this was built with its customers, not pitched at them after the fact.

## What comes next

DTCC's roadmap includes adding Stellar and Circle's Arc blockchain next year, along with broader corporate-action support and tools for settling transactions with digital cash. That last item is the one to watch. Settlement with digital cash is where tokenized securities stop being a record-keeping exercise and start changing how money actually moves.

## The skeptical read

A few caveats before anyone declares victory.

First, the $30 trillion figure is the market value of the 34 eligible securities, not the amount being tokenized. It describes the ceiling, not the floor. How much actually moves onchain at launch, DTCC did not say.

Second, this is a permissioned system. Tokens move between registered wallets, under DTCC's rules, backed by custody at its own subsidiary. This is Wall Street adopting blockchain as better database software, not embracing open networks. If your interest in crypto is the permissionless kind, this is a different religion.

Third, none of this touches Bitcoin directly. But it does close out the old argument about whether the underlying technology has any place in mainstream finance. That debate is done. The live question now is whose version of the technology wins: open networks, or walled gardens run by the incumbents. I know which one the incumbents are building, and they have a $114 trillion head start.
