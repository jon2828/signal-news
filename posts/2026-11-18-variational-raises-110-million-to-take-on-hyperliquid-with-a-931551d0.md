Variational, a Cayman Islands-based crypto derivatives startup, has raised $110 million in a Series B led by Bain Capital Crypto at a $1.5 billion valuation. Dragonfly, HSG and Peak XV also participated, [according to CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/18/a-usd1-5-billion-crypto-startup-is-taking-on-hyperliquid-with-a-wall-street-playbook).

The raise is a direct shot at Hyperliquid, and the interesting part is how Variational plans to fight. Hyperliquid runs an exchange that matches buyers and sellers. Variational skips that model entirely. Its own in-house market maker quotes prices directly to traders across more than 590 markets, using a request-for-quote system, with liquidity sourced from crypto exchanges and traditional financial institutions.

The company says it has processed more than $475 billion in trading volume since launching in 2025. More than half of its daily activity now comes from traditional markets like stocks and commodities, not tokens.

Haseeb Qureshi, managing partner at Dragonfly, told CoinDesk that open interest, trading volume, active users and listed markets have all grown substantially over the past six months. "It's clear the RFQ model they popularized is working, especially for RWA trading," he said, adding that the category accounts for over 50% of volume.

## The catch

Variational's Omni platform charges no trading commissions. That sounds generous until you read a little further. Traders still face bid-ask spreads and swap financing costs, which is how the market maker gets paid. "No commissions" is a label, not free trading.

The bigger issue is counterparty risk. The in-house market maker is the platform's sole liquidity provider, and if it becomes insolvent, customers could lose their trading profits. That is not a footnote I dug out of a terms-of-service document. CoinDesk puts it in the article's own summary.

## My read

I keep coming back to the structure. When one company runs the platform and the only market maker on it, every trader's counterparty is the house. That concentrates risk in a way an exchange full of competing market makers does not. How much of that risk gets hedged away through the outside liquidity Variational taps, the article does not say, and I would want an answer before keeping serious money there.

The growth is real and the investor list is serious. Bain Capital Crypto does not lead $110 million rounds on vibes. But the pitch, that onchain trading should look more like a traditional brokerage, cuts both ways. Brokerages come with broker risk. I have watched too many crypto platforms blow up to shrug when a company's own trading desk sits on the other side of every customer trade.

Whether traders care while volumes climb is another question. The reported numbers say they do not, at least not yet.
