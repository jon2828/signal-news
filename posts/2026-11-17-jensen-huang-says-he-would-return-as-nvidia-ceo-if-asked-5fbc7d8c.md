Jensen Huang says he would return as Nvidia CEO if the board decides it wants him back, his first public statement since resigning over reports of multiple affairs, including one with a former executive suspected of working with Chinese intelligence.

The statement, posted in full on X, thanks supporters, admits to "serious mistakes in my personal life," and pledges cooperation with the board's independent investigation. Then he gets to the point: employees, investors, partners, and customers have been calling for his return, Huang says, and if the board decides that serves the company's best interests, he "would be honored" to answer.

I have read a lot of founder apology statements. This one is a job application wearing an apology's clothes.

## The campaign is already running

Huang is not waiting by the phone. There is an organized push behind him. An open letter circulating this week collected more than 10,000 signatures from current and former Nvidia employees, plus tech figures like Elon Musk, Amjad Masad, and Travis Kalanick.

The letter's argument is about process, not innocence. It says Huang was "removed without meaningful due process" and demands the board give "a specific, evidence-based explanation" if his absence continues.

That framing is clever. It lets people support Huang without defending the affairs or the Alice Huang connection. I suspect plenty of those 10,000 signatures are really about the stock and the company's direction. Nvidia without Jensen is a frightening thought if you hold the shares.

## The case against

The board does not sound moved. A source on Nvidia's board told The Washington Post on Monday there are no plans to reinstate him.

Congress is the bigger wall. Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress on December 1, and Nvidia's chip exports to China-linked firms face intense scrutiny. The Alice Huang allegation sits directly on top of the most sensitive issue the company has. One senator, reached by Politico, called reinstatement "one of the worst ideas I've heard today."

Nvidia has also formed a new executive committee to oversee export control compliance in Huang's absence. Read that as a message to Washington: the adults are in the room now. Bringing Huang back before his testimony would shred that message.

## What I think

Huang founded Nvidia and built it into what he calls a "once-in-a-generation company," and the loyalty around him is real. But read the structure of his statement. One paragraph owns his mistakes. The next lists everyone who wants him back. That is not contrition. That is a whip count.

The board has an open investigation and a founder publicly volunteering to return before it finishes. Nobody outside that boardroom knows what the investigation will find. A comeback campaign this loud is a bet that pressure outruns evidence. It might. I would not take that bet.
