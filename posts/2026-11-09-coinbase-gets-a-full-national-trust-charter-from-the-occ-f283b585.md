Coinbase now runs a fully chartered national trust bank. The company said Monday that the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency upgraded its status from conditional to full, capping a process that began with a provisional approval in April. CoinDesk's Jesse Hamilton reports the final sign‑off came after months of building out compliance systems and hiring the people to run them, all under ongoing regulatory scrutiny.

## What the charter does and doesn't do

The national trust charter lets Coinbase offer federally regulated custodial and payments services. It does not turn the exchange into a full‑service bank. Taking deposits and extending loans stay off the table.

That distinction is the part worth watching. "Trust bank" sounds like "bank," and plenty of people will hear it that way. What Coinbase actually got is a federal wrapper around custody and payments - real, but narrower than the headline implies. My read: for an exchange, custody and payments are close to the whole business, so the charter still covers the parts that matter most to Coinbase.

## The stablecoin pipeline

Coinbase, like Ripple, is positioned to benefit from the new framework. Ripple received its own full trust charter from the OCC last month, and a line of other firms holds provisional approvals. The rush picked up after the Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act, shortened to the GENIUS Act, cleared a path for federal trusts to issue compliant stablecoins.

The OCC under the Trump administration has been openly warm to crypto applicants. One of those applicants is World Liberty Financial, the Trump family's own venture, which picked up a conditional trust status from the same agency.

## My take

I keep coming back to the optics. The regulator issuing these charters works under an administration whose family owns one of the firms in line. That doesn't make Coinbase's charter illegitimate. The company spent months on compliance and hiring to earn the upgrade, and the biggest U.S. crypto exchange was always going to be near the front of this line. But when the president's family holds a conditional version of the same prize, every approval the OCC hands out now carries a question mark it didn't create on its own.

There's also the irony nobody at the exchange will mention. Bitcoin exists because people wanted money that routes around trusted third parties. The biggest U.S. on‑ramp is now becoming a trusted third party with a federal seal. Maybe that's growing up. Maybe that's capture. I genuinely don't know which, and I don't think anyone else does yet.
