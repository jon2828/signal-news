Anthropic filed to go public on Monday morning, and the S-1 puts the numbers behind Claude in an SEC filing for the first time. The headline figures: $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026, against a net loss of $80.7 billion.

Read that again. The loss is almost five times the revenue.

## The numbers

Share count and price range are still blank in the filing, so the valuation talk is coming from elsewhere. The FT reports investors expect $1.5 to $1.75 trillion. Earlier reports floated a raise of up to $100 billion at a $2 trillion valuation. Fox Business's Liz Claman says pricing on November 19 or 20 remains on track. The company plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT.

The balance sheet is not the problem. Anthropic held $112.4 billion in cash and equivalents plus $19.3 billion in short-term investments as of June 30. But the filing warns that infrastructure investment will increase significantly, putting pressure on future cash flows. Given the loss figure, that reads less like caution and more like a promise.

The customer numbers are genuinely strong. By September 30, more than 1,900 customers had generated at least $1 million in trailing annual revenue, and roughly 250 of those had generated more than $10 million. That is real enterprise adoption, not API hobbyists.

## The two quotes worth reading

The prospectus opens with the mission language you would expect. "We believe that AI is one of the most consequential technologies in human history," the company says, promising faster scientific progress and cures for major diseases.

Then the risk factors arrive. Advanced AI systems, the filing warns, could "pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" if misused, weaponized, or allowed to operate with a high degree of autonomy.

I keep coming back to the fact that both of these statements sit in the same document, aimed at the same potential shareholders. Buy the stock, and also, the product might end the world. Anthropic has always been the lab that says the quiet part loud, and now it is doing it in a registration statement. At least they are consistent.

The governance structure matches the posture. Anthropic is going public as a Delaware public benefit corporation, with a Long-Term Benefit Trust of "financially disinterested trustees" meant to keep management balanced against its public benefit purpose. Whether that structure survives contact with public market pressure is a real question, and nobody knows the answer.

## The market reaction

Chip stocks rose in premarket trading after the filing: Nvidia up 1.4%, Broadcom up 1.5%, Micron up 2.3%. The market's read is simple. Anthropic's losses are Nvidia's revenue.

The stranger reaction happened on Hyperliquid, the crypto exchange where traders can already bet on Anthropic's expected share price through a contract called ANTH. It spiked to about $1,980 around the 9 a.m. ET filing time, then dropped roughly 6% to about $1,857 within minutes. Around $12 million changed hands in 24 hours. A pre-IPO price discovery market on a crypto exchange, reacting to an S-1 in real time, is a very 2026 sentence.

## My read

The revenue is real and growing fast. The loss is also real and growing faster. Anthropic is asking public investors to fund the gap between those two lines at a valuation somewhere north of a trillion dollars, while warning them in writing that the technology could pose existential risks.

Maybe that is the honest way to do this. Maybe it is absurd. Probably both. Pricing is expected in mid-November, and I will be watching what the ANTH contract does between now and then.
