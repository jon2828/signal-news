Jensen Huang has pulled out of the ElevenLabs Summit in New York, and it is not a scheduling conflict. ElevenLabs confirmed Monday that the Nvidia CEO will no longer appear at its event on Wednesday, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-pulls-out-of-elevenlabs-summit-amid-nvidia-controversy/). He has also dropped other speaking engagements, including the Anthropic CEO Forum.

The withdrawals follow a brutal weekend. Huang admitted to an extramarital affair in a memo to the company, writing that he was "unfaithful to my wife" and that there is "no excuse for what I did." Around the same time, Nvidia opened an investigation into his relationship with a possible Chinese spy.

## Two scandals, one CEO

The affair is a private mess playing out in public. Ugly, but survivable on its own. Executives have apologized for worse and kept their jobs.

The investigation is the part that matters. Nvidia has China ambitions, per the report, and a CEO under investigation over a relationship with a possible Chinese spy runs straight into them. I would not want to be the Nvidia lawyer fielding calls this week. Any partner or regulator with a security concern now has a new question to ask, and Nvidia has no good answer until the investigation ends.

One detail worth noting: Nvidia is an investor in ElevenLabs. Pulling out of an event hosted by a portfolio company is a stronger signal than skipping a random conference. Huang's team wants him out of public view, and they are willing to spend some goodwill to make that happen fast.

## What happens to Huang

TechCrunch says the episode has rattled the AI industry and raised questions about Huang's staying power as CEO. That tracks with how these things usually go. The first 48 hours are about apologies. The weeks after are about the board, and boards move slowly until they move all at once.

I keep coming back to how much of Nvidia's public identity is Huang himself. That cuts both ways now. If the investigation clears him, the affair reads as a personal failure he has already apologized for, and the story fades. If it does not, Nvidia has a problem with no clean fix, and I do not know what that version of the company looks like.

Nobody outside Nvidia knows what the investigation will find. The summit circuit goes on without him this week. Everything else is wait and see.
