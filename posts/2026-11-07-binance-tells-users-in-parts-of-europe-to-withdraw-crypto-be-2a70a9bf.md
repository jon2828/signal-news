Binance is emailing users in several European countries telling them to pull their crypto off the exchange, with monthly fees on leftover balances starting Nov. 29 and affected accounts closing on Dec. 31, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/07/binance-users-in-some-european-countries-told-to-pull-crypto-before-fees-kick-in-with-accounts-closing-by-year-end).

Users in Spain, France, Italy and Poland say they received the notices, and people on X have been sharing the emails. Deposits are already blocked for affected accounts. Withdrawals still work.

A Binance spokesperson told CoinDesk the exchange is "progressively restricting certain services for affected users" following the end of the transition period under the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) rules. The spokesperson said "affected accounts will be closed with effect from 31 December 2026," called it "a carefully managed process," and insisted users' assets remain safe and accessible with "no sudden freeze."

## The fee is the part worth reading

The fee structure is where this gets sharp. Starting Nov. 29, Binance will charge 0.025% of whatever crypto balance remains each month, or 10 USDC, whichever is higher. The first charge lands within three days of Nov. 29. A second comes after Dec. 24. And if your balance is smaller than the fee, Binance takes the whole thing.

That last clause is the one I keep coming back to. A 0.025% monthly fee sounds trivial. A 10 USDC minimum that drains any balance below it is not trivial at all for small holders, who are exactly the people least likely to open an email from an exchange they stopped using. If you left 8 USDC of dust sitting on Binance in Madrid and you miss the deadline, Binance keeps it.

To be fair, the exchange is giving notice, and withdrawals are still open. But "carefully managed process" is doing a lot of work in that statement. From the user's side, the process looks like this: deposits blocked, account dead at year end, and a fee schedule that eats small balances whole if you don't act in time.

## The MiCA problem

The root cause is regulatory. Binance still has no MiCA license, which it needs to serve customers anywhere in the EU.

This didn't come out of nowhere. Binance first contacted users in France, Spain, Italy and Poland back in June, urging them to withdraw. Those are the countries where it ran locally registered businesses before MiCA's transition period ended. Users in countries where Binance had no local entity, like Portugal, haven't been singled out the same way, though they remain subject to the broader EU restrictions, according to Portuguese newspaper Expresso.

What the reporting doesn't answer is what happens to crypto still sitting in these accounts after Dec. 31. The fee schedule suggests Binance expects stragglers. Whether those users eventually get their funds back, or the balances just get absorbed, is an open question.

If you're in one of the affected countries and you have anything on Binance, the move is obvious: withdraw now, not on Dec. 30.
