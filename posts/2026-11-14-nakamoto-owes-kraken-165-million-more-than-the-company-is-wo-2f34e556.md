# Nakamoto owes Kraken $165 million, more than the company is worth

David Bailey's bitcoin treasury company Nakamoto (NAKA) owes Kraken 165 million USDT, a debt larger than the company's entire market value, and this week it pushed a $60 million repayment on that loan back by a full year.

The $60 million was due Dec. 4. Under an amendment signed Wednesday and disclosed in an 8‑K filing Thursday, it now matures Dec. 4, 2027. The remaining 105 million USDT is still due June 30, 2027, which leaves Nakamoto with no scheduled principal repayments this year. The loan is denominated in the USDT stablecoin and secured by bitcoin.

Kraken also agreed to count the net value of Nakamoto's bitcoin derivatives positions, including put options that pay out if BTC falls, toward the collateral requirements. Tyler Evans, Nakamoto's chief investment officer, said the amendment "extends our runway and makes our collateral position more resilient."

## The cushion is thinner than it looks

Runway is one word for it. Here is the math, per Nakamoto's quarterly report filed this week. At the end of September, 3,030 of the company's 4,283 bitcoin were pledged to Kraken. With bitcoin near $83,500 then, those coins were worth about $253 million.

Bitcoin has since slid. At Friday's price under $69,000, the pledged coins are worth roughly $208 million, about 126 % of what Nakamoto owes, according to CoinDesk's calculations.

That 126 % is the number to watch. If the collateral value drops below a set maintenance level, Nakamoto has to repay part of the loan, pledge more bitcoin, or both. The company has about 1,253 unpledged bitcoin left, worth roughly $86 million at Friday's price. That is the entire buffer between "extends our runway" and a margin call.

## One more wrinkle

The put options Kraken now counts toward collateral expire before the debt comes due. The protection runs out before the bill does.

## The treasury model meets gravity

I keep coming back to the market‑value detail, because it reframes everything else in the filing. A lender is owed more than the borrower is worth, and the company's plan is to buy time. Waiting is the plan because the alternative, selling bitcoin into a falling market to service a bitcoin‑collateralized loan, is the exact doom loop these treasury companies were pitched as being immune to.

To be fair, Nakamoto is not insolvent on paper. The collateral still covers the loan, and the amendment does buy time. But time is only useful if bitcoin cooperates, and bitcoin has dropped from near $83,500 to under $69,000 since the end of September. Every further leg down burns through the 126 % coverage and drags the maintenance trigger closer, with only that $86 million in unpledged coins standing by.

The filing does not disclose the exact maintenance trigger level, so its precise location is unknown to the public. What we do know is the direction of travel: the collateral is shrinking, the reserve is finite, and the hedge expires early.
