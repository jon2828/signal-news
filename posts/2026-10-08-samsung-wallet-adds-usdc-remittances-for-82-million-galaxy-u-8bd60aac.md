Samsung is turning Samsung Wallet into a cross-border remittance tool, with eligible US Galaxy users able to send USDC abroad starting in the last week of October, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/08/solana-partners-with-samsung-to-integrate-usdc-in-82-million-phones).

## What Samsung is actually launching

The feature covers 82 million Galaxy devices across the United States. Users will be able to send USDC to compatible crypto wallets abroad, or send local-currency payouts to eligible bank accounts in more than 60 countries. Recipients may receive local currency without opening a crypto wallet at all.

The plumbing comes from a stack of partners. Solana and Sui provide the underlying blockchain support. Bastion custodies the stablecoin through Coinbase Prime Vault and supplies the regulated stablecoin infrastructure, with Coinbase acting as sub-custodian.

On fees, Samsung says it will not charge for transfers to compatible external wallets, though third-party fees may apply.

"Sending money abroad should feel as convenient as using the wallet already on your phone," Woncheol Chai, head of Samsung Electronics' digital-wallet team, said. He framed the pitch around getting started "without installing another app or managing private keys themselves."

## My take

The interesting part is what's missing: the crypto. No seed phrases. No new app. Samsung is betting that stablecoins go mainstream by disappearing into software people already use for cards, IDs and boarding passes, and I think that bet is directionally right. This is the largest test of that theory I can point to.

Now the skeptical notes. "Eligible users" is doing a lot of work in this announcement, and the report doesn't spell out what eligibility actually means. The fee picture is also murkier than the headline suggests. Samsung waiving its own fee on wallet-to-wallet transfers tells you nothing about what Bastion, Coinbase, or the payout partners charge on the bank-account side. "Third-party fees may apply" is historically where remittance costs go to hide.

Worth saying plainly: this is a stablecoin story, not a Bitcoin story. USDC on Solana and Sui, custodied by Coinbase. Users hold a claim on a custodian, not coins. That is a reasonable design for payments, but it is the opposite of self-custody, and anyone reading this as validation for Bitcoin should recalibrate.

Whether people actually want to remit stablecoins, rather than just dollars that happen to settle on a blockchain, remains the open question. Samsung now has 82 million devices to find out with.
