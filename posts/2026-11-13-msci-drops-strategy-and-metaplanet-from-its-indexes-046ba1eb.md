MSCI is removing Strategy from its USA Index and Metaplanet from its Japan Small Cap Index at the close of Nov. 30, according to filings published Wednesday and [reported by The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-11-strategy-metaplanet-lose-msci-index-membership-november-reshuffle-438437). Both are bitcoin treasury companies, and both removals were mostly expected.

## What changed

The deletions follow eligibility rules MSCI adopted last month for what it calls non-operating companies. The new screen looks at a company's operating assets and five financial measures. It replaces a temporary restriction on firms holding at least half their assets in crypto, and MSCI had warned that companies failing the screen across their two most recent annual filing periods would be removed.

The review documents gave no company-specific reasons for the removals. One detail worth noting: Sharplink, an Ethereum treasury company, was not among the November small-cap deletions. Whatever line MSCI is drawing, it is not a simple "holds crypto" line.

## Strategy fought the rule and lost

Strategy Executive Chairman Michael Saylor and Phong Le had publicly pushed back, calling the proposed treatment of digital asset treasuries "discriminatory." In a September letter, Strategy argued that exclusion would have no meaningful impact on its business but would damage MSCI's reputation as a neutral index provider.

I have never fully bought the "no meaningful impact" line, and the market does not seem to either. MSTR fell 2.6% in after-hours trading to $119.06 after the filings came out. Metaplanet had already closed Wednesday down 2.53% at 231 yen in Tokyo, before the announcement landed.

A 2.6% dip is not a collapse. But index membership is plumbing, not symbolism. Funds built to track the MSCI USA Index will have to sell MSTR when the removal takes effect, and that selling happens regardless of what anyone thinks about bitcoin. The Block's newsletter flagged exactly this, pointing to concerns over "potential selling pressure" from the removal as one factor in Thursday's market drop.

## The timing could be better

Bitcoin fell to a 12-week low of $71,200 on Thursday, a move [The Block attributed](https://www.theblock.co/newsletters/the-daily/2026-11-12-bitcoin-12-week-low-strategy-metaplanet-msci-more-crypto-news-439049) to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation and to MSCI-related selling worries. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO, and its shares opened down 12%. Bitcoin was down 3.4% at around $71,600 at the time of reporting, and crypto liquidations topped $750 million, with more than $634 million of that in long positions, per CoinGlass.

Wintermute OTC trader Jasper De Maere said his desk remains cautious as bitcoin tests the $73,000 level, seeing "no convincing signs of size stepping in at these prices."

Meanwhile, Strategy is still buying. The company [picked up another 1,036 bitcoin for $82 million](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-09-connecting-the-dots-michael-saylor-strategy-bitcoin-436094) earlier this week, taking its holdings past 850,000 BTC. Saylor is not slowing down because an index provider showed him the door.

## My read

Calling the rule "discriminatory" was always a stretch. Index providers decide what counts as an operating company, and a firm whose balance sheet is mostly bitcoin is a fair subject for that debate. Strategy can dislike the answer without the question being illegitimate.

That said, MSCI gave no specific explanation for either removal, and that is weak. If you are going to eject the most visible corporate bitcoin holder in the world from your main country index, say exactly why. The open question now is whether other index providers follow MSCI's lead or carve their own path. Nobody knows yet, and the answer matters for how easily Strategy can keep doing the one thing it does, which is raising money to buy bitcoin.
