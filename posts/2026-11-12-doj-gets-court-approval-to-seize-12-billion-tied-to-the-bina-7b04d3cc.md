The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authority to seize more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency frozen after the October 29 attack on Binance, a theft the FBI has attributed to a North Korean military-linked hacking operation known as TraderTraitor.

The numbers are worth sitting with. More than $6 billion was stolen in the hack. U.S. authorities and private companies have frozen more than $2.7 billion of it so far, according to the DOJ, and investigators are still tracing funds moving through blockchain bridges, mixers and exchanges.

M. Scott Proctor, principal deputy assistant attorney general for national security, said North Korea's government "bankrolls its rogue regime and illicit weapons program through cryptocurrency theft," and said the National Security Division will use every tool available to seize and return the funds.

## The deepfake angle

The same announcement covered a second operation, and this is the part I keep stopping on. Federal agents seized cloud infrastructure used in a North Korean campaign called FaceStealer, which the FBI says has stolen more than $400 million in crypto over the past 18 months.

The scheme is simple and nasty. Victims get invited to what looks like a Zoom or Microsoft Teams call. The person inviting them looks and sounds like someone they trust, because the attackers replay secretly recorded webcam footage from earlier victims or, increasingly, generate deepfake video with AI. Once the target joins the fake meeting, the attackers get them to install malware.

Deepfake scams keep improving, but the detail that gets me is the recycled webcam footage. Every successful attack produces raw material for the next one. The victim pool becomes the prop department.

## What to make of it

The $1.2 billion seizure is real money, and freezing $2.7 billion of a $6 billion theft in about two weeks is a faster response than crypto enforcement used to manage. Credit where due.

But more than $3 billion is still out there, moving through the same bridges and mixers that make this kind of laundering so hard to stop. Seizures after the fact are not a defense. They are cleanup.

The FaceStealer numbers point the other direction. $400 million in 18 months, with AI-generated video making the fake meetings more convincing over time. The DOJ can seize servers. It cannot make people stop trusting their own eyes on a video call, and that trust is the actual vulnerability being exploited.

Nobody knows how much of the remaining Binance money comes back. The FaceStealer campaign is the part of this story I would watch. The dollar figure is smaller, and the technique is the thing that scales.
