Nvidia's board is preparing to oust founder and CEO Jensen Huang after two extramarital affairs came to light, one of them with a woman US intelligence officials believe was spying for Beijing, The Wall Street Journal reported. Directors could meet as soon as Wednesday to determine his future, according to people familiar with the matter cited in the report.

Most CEO scandals are gossip dressed up as news. This one is not, and the reason why matters for anyone holding AI stocks or Bitcoin.

## What the reports say

The Journal's report, relayed by Cointelegraph, says the board is ready to move against Huang after the two affairs surfaced. The first involved Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data company and a major Nvidia customer. US intelligence officials believe she was a spy for Beijing.

The New York Times reported Wednesday that Huang also had an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, according to five people who worked at the company.

On Sunday, Huang acknowledged the infidelity in a letter to employees. He wrote that he "was unfaithful to my wife" and that "there is no excuse for what I did." He confirmed the board had launched an independent investigation into national security concerns raised in news reports, pledged to cooperate fully, and said he remains committed to the company.

Nvidia did not respond to Cointelegraph's request for comment.

## Why this one is different

A CEO's marriage is normally none of my business. Two consenting adults, a private failure, an HR problem at most. But one of these relationships was with an executive at a major customer whom US intelligence believes worked for Beijing, and Huang runs the company that has become central to the AI boom. If that assessment is right, this is not a tabloid story. It is a counterintelligence problem in the corner office.

Boards do not move fast unless they feel they have to. Huang founded Nvidia and built it into the company at the center of the AI trade, and directors are still reportedly ready to meet within days to discuss removing him. That tells you how seriously they are taking the security question, or how badly they want distance from it. Probably both.

I want to be careful with the certainty here. "Believed to be a spy" is not "convicted of espionage." The reporting is hours old, Nvidia has not commented, and Huang says he is cooperating. Nobody outside that boardroom knows how this plays out.

## The market reaction

Traders did not wait for the board. Nvidia shares fell 8% to $159.76 in after-hours trading following the reports, reversing a 2.9% gain from the regular session, according to Google Finance data cited by Cointelegraph. The story spread fast, too. Watcher.Guru's post on X about the Journal's report racked up 8.9 million views.

## The Bitcoin angle

Cointelegraph's related coverage notes that Bitcoin traders are watching the Nvidia volatility, with BTC eyeing $73,000. When the company at the center of the AI trade drops 8% after hours, risk assets everywhere feel it. Whether Bitcoin shrugs this off or follows Nvidia down is a real question for the week, and I would not trust anyone who claims to know the answer tonight.

The board could meet as soon as Wednesday. By the time you read this, Huang may be out, or the whole thing may have gone quiet. Either way, "suspected Chinese spy" and "Nvidia CEO" now share a headline. That story does not go away quietly.
