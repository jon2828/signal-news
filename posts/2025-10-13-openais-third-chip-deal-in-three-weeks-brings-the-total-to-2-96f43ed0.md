OpenAI and Broadcom announced a deal on Monday to deploy 10 gigawatts of custom AI accelerators, with OpenAI designing the chips and systems and Broadcom co-developing and deploying the racks, all networked with Broadcom's Ethernet and connectivity gear. It is the third gigawatt-scale announcement from OpenAI in three weeks, and it brings the company's announced total to 26 gigawatts of future compute.

## Three deals, one pattern

The sequence is worth laying out:

- September 22: NVIDIA and OpenAI announced a letter of intent for at least 10 gigawatts of NVIDIA systems, with NVIDIA intending to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI as each gigawatt gets deployed. The first gigawatt is targeted for the second half of 2026 on the Vera Rubin platform.
- October 6: AMD and OpenAI announced a definitive 6 gigawatt agreement across multiple generations of Instinct GPUs, starting with 1 gigawatt of MI450 chips in the second half of 2026. AMD also issued OpenAI a warrant for up to 160 million shares, vesting as purchases scale and as AMD hits certain share-price targets.
- October 13: Broadcom and OpenAI announced a term sheet for 10 gigawatts of OpenAI-designed accelerators, with deployments starting in the second half of 2026 and completing by the end of 2029.

Notice the date that keeps coming up. Everything starts in the back half of 2026. What exists today is a letter of intent, a definitive agreement, and a term sheet. The hardware itself is still a promise.

## The money goes in circles

I keep coming back to the structure of these deals, because the structure is the story.

NVIDIA plans to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI, and OpenAI plans to spend it on NVIDIA systems. AMD is handing OpenAI up to 160 million shares that vest as OpenAI buys more AMD GPUs. The chipmakers fund or pay the customer, the customer commits to buying the chips, and both sides get a Monday headline. Broadcom's release, to its credit, mentions no investment or equity sweetener, just chips and networking. Make of that what you will.

Sam Altman's line in the NVIDIA release was "Everything starts with compute." But compute starts with electricity and money, and 26 gigawatts requires an enormous amount of both. None of the three announcements says where the electricity comes from.

One small detail I enjoyed: the NVIDIA release in September cited 700 million weekly active users for OpenAI. The Broadcom release, three weeks later, cites 800 million. Either growth is vertical or someone rounds up when there is a deal to sell.

## What I'd watch

Nobody knows how much of this gets built. Term sheets and letters of intent are not purchase orders, and even the definitive AMD agreement stages everything through milestones that run to the end of the decade. The second half of 2026 is when the press releases meet the power grid. Until the first racks actually power on, I would treat 26 gigawatts as an aspiration with very good PR.
