Schroders, the London-based asset manager overseeing $1.1 trillion, is bringing an emerging markets bond fund to Solana, its first deployment on the network, [CoinDesk reported Wednesday](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/04/global-asset-manager-schroders-brings-emerging-market-bond-fund-to-solana).

The firm, founded more than 200 years ago, is working with tokenization specialist Centrifuge to put its Schroder International Selection Fund Emerging Market Bonds strategy onchain. The structure is a feeder fund: investors hold tokenized shares that buy into Schroders' underlying bond fund, with share values tracking net asset value. The strategy holds debt issued by governments and companies across emerging economies. Schroders has managed emerging markets debt for over 50 years and has more than 40 investment professionals dedicated to the asset class.

## Tokenization grows past cash

Most of the tokenization boom so far has been cash in a new wrapper. Tokenized U.S. Treasuries and money market funds make up $14 billion of the $39 billion tokenized real-world asset market tracked by RWA.xyz. Those products took off because they let investors earn yield on cash while moving it across blockchain networks.

An actively managed emerging markets bond fund is a different animal. Returns are tied to the creditworthiness of borrowers across emerging economies, which is a long way from a tokenized T-bill. You are underwriting governments and companies, not parking cash.

Schroders is not the only big name moving this way. CoinDesk notes the launch follows recent tokenization moves by Federated Hermes and New York Life's asset management arm.

## My take

The Treasury tokenization pitch was easy to grasp. Park cash onchain, earn yield, move it fast. EM bonds are a harder sell. The natural buyers for this fund are institutions, and institutions already have rails. Tokenizing the shares does not change the credit risk, the currency risk, or the liquidity of the underlying debt. It changes the wrapper.

The wrapper might still be the point. If every fund eventually gets an onchain share class, the chain decisions being made right now matter. Schroders picked Solana for its first one. A firm with $1.1 trillion and two centuries behind it does not make that call by accident.

Whether demand shows up is the open question. A tokenized feeder fund is still a fund with a gatekeeper, and nobody knows yet whether onchain distribution reaches buyers the old rails could not, or whether this sits as a small pilot forever. I lean skeptical on near-term demand. But the direction is clear: tokenization is moving from cash equivalents into products with real risk. That is when this stops being a plumbing story.
