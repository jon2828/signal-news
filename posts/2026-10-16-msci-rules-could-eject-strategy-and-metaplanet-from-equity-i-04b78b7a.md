MSCI finalized rules on Thursday that could remove Strategy and Metaplanet, the leading bitcoin-holding companies, from its global equity indexes. The rules, first proposed in August, make companies that mostly hold investments rather than running a real business ineligible for MSCI's Global Investable Market Indexes, [CoinDesk reports](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/16/msci-approves-rules-that-could-eject-strategy-metaplanet-from-equity-indexes).

The timeline is now set. MSCI will name the affected firms on Nov. 11, and the change takes effect at the close of trading on Nov. 30.

## Why this matters more than it looks

Index inclusion is not a vanity metric. When a company sits in an MSCI index, every fund tracking that index has to own its stock. When it gets removed, those funds have to sell, whatever the managers think of the company. The buying and selling is mechanical.

JPMorgan analysts estimate that Strategy's exclusion alone could trigger about $2.8 billion in stock outflows. That is forced selling aimed at a stock that last closed at $136.83, and it arrives whether or not anyone has changed their mind about bitcoin.

One detail worth keeping straight: the forced sales would hit Strategy's shares, not its bitcoin. Nobody is making the company dump its holdings. This is about who gets to own the stock passively, not about what sits on the balance sheet.

## The reflexivity problem

I keep coming back to how much of the treasury company model runs on passive flows. The pitch works like this: hold bitcoin, get into indexes, let index funds bid up your shares, then issue more shares at a premium and buy more bitcoin. It is a tidy loop while it runs. It also depends entirely on index providers agreeing you are a business rather than a fund.

MSCI is now saying the quiet part out loud. If your balance sheet is mostly investments, you look like an investment vehicle, and investment vehicles do not belong in equity indexes built to track operating companies. That logic is hard to argue with on the merits, even if the timing feels hostile.

ZEROBASE, a decentralized infrastructure network, framed it on X as "a growing tension" between traditional index construction and companies that treat bitcoin as a core treasury asset rather than a secondary holding. The firm also noted that passive capital has become an important source of demand for these stocks. That is a polite way of saying the demand was never fully voluntary.

## What to watch

The Nov. 11 list is the next checkpoint. Strategy and Metaplanet are the obvious candidates, but the rules are written generally, so other investment-heavy firms could show up too.

The open question is whether other index providers follow MSCI's lead. If this stays an MSCI-only decision, the damage is roughly the JPMorgan number plus some pain in the share price. If it spreads, the treasury company trade loses one of its main engines. Nobody knows which way that goes yet.
