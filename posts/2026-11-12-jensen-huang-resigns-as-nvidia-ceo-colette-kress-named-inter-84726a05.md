Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO, and board member of Nvidia, effective immediately, days after admitting to a romantic affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Nvidia's board accepted the resignation Wednesday, [per a regulatory filing](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/nvidias-jensen-huang-resigns-chipmaker-names-cfo-colette-kress-as-interim-ceo/), and CFO Colette Kress takes over as interim CEO while keeping the finance job.

This ends a 33-year run. Huang founded Nvidia in 1993 and led it the entire time. He was so central to the company that he never set up a succession plan, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-is-out-at-nvidia-who-is-colette-kress-his-interim-replacement/), which means the board is now improvising at a company worth about $3.8 trillion.

## The market reaction

Investors are not taking it well. Nvidia shares fell nearly 12% in early Thursday trading. Since Friday, the stock has dropped as much as 19%, a market cap loss of more than $855 billion.

The shock hit Nvidia's orbit too. CoreWeave fell about 9% Thursday, giving back part of a 25% rally from Wednesday. Nebius dropped about 16%. IREN fell 6.6%.

## Why this scandal, why now

The details are ugly. TechCrunch reports Huang had affairs with both an alleged Chinese spy and one of his direct reports, a disclosure that set off what the company coverage describes as a U.S. national security crisis.

For most CEOs this would be a tabloid story. For Huang it is geopolitical. He spent years lobbying Washington to loosen the export controls on AI chips sold to China, and China still accounted for about $7.9 billion of Nvidia's revenue last quarter, roughly 8% of the total. An intimate relationship with an alleged Chinese spy is close to the worst imaginable scandal for the man in that position.

## Who is Colette Kress

Kress has been at Nvidia since September 2013. Before that she spent three years as CFO of Cisco's Business Technology Operations Finance group and 13 years at Microsoft, four of them as CFO of the Server and Tools division. She is 59, holds a finance degree from the University of Arizona and an MBA from Southern Methodist, and Forbes estimates her net worth at about $1.2 billion. One nice detail from her Forbes profile: she joined Nvidia partly because her two sons liked what it did for video games.

Analysts know her from earnings calls. Her first statement was what you would expect from an interim chief, saying she is "committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created." The real introduction comes Tuesday, when Nvidia reports quarterly earnings and she takes questions.

## The part that actually matters

The affair is the headline. The money is the story.

Huang turned Nvidia into the AI boom's bank. As of the end of its second quarter, the company reported $99 billion in investments plus another $25 billion in commitments, along with $36 billion in cloud service commitments. It put $30 billion into OpenAI, $10 billion into Anthropic, $2 billion each into CoreWeave and Nebius, and signed up for as much as $105 billion in credit guarantees for an OpenAI data center campus in Ohio.

Much of that money flows back to Nvidia as chip purchases. The company finances its own customers, and Huang was the architect and salesman of that arrangement, the person who made counterparties and Washington comfortable with it. Now he is gone, there is no permanent replacement, and the interim CEO is a finance chief whose job has been counting the money rather than deploying it.

I don't know whether Kress keeps writing those checks, slows them down, or starts unwinding them. Neither does anyone else, and that uncertainty now hangs over every neocloud and AI lab that assumed Nvidia's patronage was a constant. Tuesday's earnings call just became the most interesting one of the year.
