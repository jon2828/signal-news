The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance said Tuesday it views Canton Coin (CC) as a digital commodity and will not recommend enforcement action over the token's programmatic issuance, according to a no-action letter [reported by The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-03-sec-no-action-letter-canton-coin-digital-commodity-432492).

## What the letter says

The staff's reasoning comes down to where CC's value comes from. The division said the token derives value from the programmatic operation of the Canton Network and from supply-and-demand dynamics, "rather than expectations of profit from the managerial efforts of others." It also cited the network's lack of a central party.

Deputy Chief Counsel Jonathan Ingram wrote that the division "will not recommend enforcement action to the Commission" if CC is minted programmatically by Super Validators, Validators and application providers, under the circumstances Canton described, without registration under Section 5 of the Securities Act or Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act.

Now the part that matters more than the headline. The letter is an enforcement position based on the specific facts and representations Canton submitted. It does not express a legal conclusion on whether CC is a security. The comfort holds as long as reality matches what Canton told the staff. That is a narrow promise covering one token on one network, not a ruling.

## The Canton context

Canton Network is a blockchain infrastructure protocol developed by Digital Asset, built for privacy-enabled, interoperable transactions across institutions. There is real money behind it. Digital Asset raised $355 million in June in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz's a16z crypto fund, and in May 21Shares became the first U.S. ETF issuer to offer direct exposure to CC.

## The a16z reaction

Miles Jennings, a partner at a16z, called the letter "incredible" and argued on X that the SEC's token taxonomy from March and its follow-up FAQs have created "a clear pathway out of securities laws" for network tokens, which in his telling are digital commodities.

Worth remembering who is talking. a16z led the $355 million round into Digital Asset back in June. Jennings is an investor whose bet pays off if "network tokens are digital commodities" becomes the accepted reading of the rules. That does not make him wrong. It means his confidence comes with a conflict, and it should be discounted accordingly.

## My read

The letter does hand token projects a concrete example of SEC staff treating a network token as a commodity because it is minted programmatically, has no central party, and draws value from the network's operation rather than a managerial team. If you are building a token, Canton just produced a checklist.

But keep the ceiling in mind. This is staff-level comfort tied to one set of facts, not a rule or a court decision. Whether the March taxonomy plus letters like this one amount to the clear pathway Jennings describes, or whether every project still has to walk in and ask for its own relief one at a time, is genuinely unknown. I would not bet either way yet.
