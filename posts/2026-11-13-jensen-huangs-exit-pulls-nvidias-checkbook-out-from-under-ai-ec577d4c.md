# Jensen Huang's exit pulls Nvidia's checkbook out from under AI startups

**Date:** 2026-11-13

Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia's CEO after 33 years at the helm of the company he co‑founded, and AI founders are now facing a markedly different funding landscape. "It's like a bomb went off," one neocloud founder told *TechCrunch*.

### Nvidia's role in startup financing

PitchBook data cited by *TechCrunch* shows Nvidia poured **tens of billions of dollars** into startups each year and participated in **105 funding rounds** this year alone, often as the lead investor. Dawn Capital's Shamillah Bankiya explained, "Nvidia is carrying almost every AI‑native startup from application to neocloud."

### The personal touch of Jensen Huang

According to Umesh Padval, managing partner at Seligman Ventures, most of Nvidia's significant startup investments were driven directly by Huang. Padval has known Huang since serving on the board of Mellanox, the networking chipmaker Nvidia acquired for about **$7 billion in 2020**.

### Notable investments

- Nvidia backed **CoreWeave** while it was still private.
- It participated in two rounds for **Crusoe**, including the **$1.4 billion Series E**.
- The company agreed to acquire **Hugging Face** for **$13 billion**, a deal expected to close in 2027.
- Nvidia also holds stakes in **Mistral AI**, **Lambda**, and **Black Forest Labs**.
- Last year it wrote an **$800 million** check into **Reflection AI's $2 billion Series B** round, before the open‑source startup generated meaningful revenue, per the *Wall Street Journal*.

A venture capitalist told *TechCrunch* that infrastructure startups needing **$100 million‑$500 million** in GPUs often raised roughly half of that amount from Nvidia, at valuations of **$1 billion or more**. Padval added, "When Nvidia was on the cap table, everyone felt safe."

### What may change under interim CEO Colette Kress

Interim CEO Colette Kress is expected to adopt a more conservative approach to Nvidia's AI‑startup investments. "She's much more conservative," one investor said of Kress. "I don't think she's going to commit the same amount of money to keep the early‑stage ecosystem going."

Investors anticipate **fewer new bets** and **smaller checks** for existing portfolio companies, which could lead to a **softening of valuations**.

### Potential impact on the ecosystem

*TechCrunch* notes that Nvidia's dominance sometimes felt like a monopoly because its sizable investments often locked startups into using Nvidia chips and the CUDA software platform. Maria Davidson, founder of Relational Capital, warned, "If that direction changes, it'll be a big blow to so many young companies."

Bankiya also highlighted that Huang's support extended to open‑source projects and European ventures, saying the loss would create "a massive funding and ecosystem gap that no one can fill."

### Who might step in?

Dharmesh Thakker, general partner at Battery Ventures, expects big‑tech rivals to vie for Nvidia's investment role, but he doubts they will pour **hundreds of millions** into pre‑revenue infrastructure startups the way Nvidia did. Coincidentally, **Google announced two new chips** this week.

### Outlook

While the slowdown in Nvidia's startup funding is unlikely to halt the broader AI boom, many investors agree the market will feel the absence of a single, well‑known backer. Former Nvidia employee Amulya Vishwanath, now running her own VC fund, emphasized, "Founders and leaders must be held accountable," and reminded the community that the ecosystem is larger than any one individual.
