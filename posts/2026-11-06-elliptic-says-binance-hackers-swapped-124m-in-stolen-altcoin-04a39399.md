The attackers behind last week's $6.43 billion Binance breach have swapped about $124 million in stolen altcoins into bitcoin, according to [an update from blockchain analysis firm Elliptic](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-06-binance-hackers-swap-stolen-tokens-124-million-bitcoin-exchange-plans-withdrawal-reopenings-435930). That comes on top of the $1.48 billion in bitcoin stolen directly in the attack, which is being attributed to North Korea.

The laundering operation is enormous on paper. Elliptic counts more than 20,000 wallets, 16 blockchains and 65,000 transactions so far. Yet the firm estimates only a "negligible amount" has actually been cashed out.

## The money is moving, but it is not leaving

The largest conversion came from stolen XRP routed through THORChain. By early Friday, roughly $50 million in XRP had been swapped, mostly into bitcoin, with about $226 million still remaining. Nearly all of the stolen Solana has already been mixed, swapped or bridged into bitcoin.

Here is where the $6.43 billion stands. About $3.11 billion, mostly ether and bitcoin, still sits in addresses linked to the attack. Another $2.85 billion has been frozen. That leaves roughly $470 million unaccounted for, and nobody has said whether the gap is price movement, successful cash-outs or both. The Block said it reached out to the report's author, Tom Robinson, for clarification. I am glad someone is asking. On-chain forensics gets presented as exact science, and a $470 million shrug says otherwise.

Elliptic expects the attackers to convert the remaining ether into bitcoin in small batches, then try to exit through OTC brokers and Chinese money laundering networks. Cashing out is the hard part, though. A $2.85 billion freeze suggests the chokepoints are holding for now.

## The AI angle is the way in, not the way out

There has been speculation that AI agents are running the laundering, which would make a tidy story about autonomous crime. Elliptic shut that down. The laundering is "clearly automated," the firm said, but nothing it has seen shows "an AI agent making decisions."

That tracks. Scripts that split funds across thousands of wallets are old technology. You do not need a language model to write one.

The AI part of this story is more uncomfortable. Binance's own post-mortem, published Tuesday, said the attack ran for months and involved a tampered developer tool, a fake job interview and compromised internal AI tooling. That last piece is what got the attackers into Binance's wallet-signing infrastructure. AI did not launder the money. The way Binance wired AI into its internal systems helped open the door.

I keep coming back to that detail. Every company is connecting AI tools to internal systems right now, usually with broader access than the tools need. A $6.43 billion breach just turned on exactly that.

## Withdrawals are trickling back

Binance said Friday on X that it aims to reopen withdrawals for HYPE, INJ, APT, PLASMA, NEAR and LTC by the end of next week, plus all tokens on networks that are already restored, including ETH, BSC, Arbitrum and Optimism. The exchange says it has replenished virtually all of the assets stolen in the breach.

That last point matters more than the laundering chase. If it holds, users were made whole whether or not the thieves are ever caught. The open question is what happens next time, at an exchange that cannot absorb the hit.
