# CoinDCX admits $16 million loss days after CEO called breach reports a false alarm

**Date:** 2026‑10‑25

Indian crypto exchange **CoinDCX** has confirmed a loss of **15.99 million USDT** (about **$16 million**) after an attacker drained deposit wallets that the exchange had retired following its July 2025 hack. The admission was posted in a blog update early Sunday, **days after** co‑founder and CEO **Sumit Gupta** told users on X that reports of a breach sounded like a *false alarm*.

---

## What happened

CoinDCX said it detected unusual activity on Tuesday that *"had impacted our old, deprecated wallet infrastructure which had unknown vulnerabilities."* An automated sweeping service that moves funds between the exchange's wallets *"had failed for a particular period,"* causing user deposits to accumulate at an address the exchange no longer used. The attacker transferred the accumulated funds, and the exchange stated that the loss *"was absorbed by the company"* while user funds remain in segregated cold wallets. Trading, deposits and withdrawals continued as normal.

---

## Timeline of the denial and subsequent revelations

- **1:30 a.m. ET Wednesday** - **Darcy**, co‑founder of asset‑rescue firm **FlashRescue**, flagged suspicious outflows: roughly **15.99 million USDT** and **5,783.81 TRX** left CoinDCX's hot wallet and several deposit addresses at **11:35 a.m. ET Tuesday**. All funds were sent to a single Tron address, with the largest single transfer of **15,819,002 USDT** later split to deposit addresses on **Binance, OKX** and **Gate**.

- **Less than three hours later** - Gupta replied to Darcy's post: *"Thanks for highlighting but this is incorrect and CoinDCX wallets are not breached,"* adding that the addresses had been compromised in July 2025 and that *"the hackers seem to be re‑using the old wallet addresses and this sounds like a false alarm."*

- **Four minutes after Gupta's reply** - CoinDCX's official X account called the claims *"misleading"* and noted the addresses *"have not been used by CoinDCX since"* the July 2025 incident.

- **On‑chain analyst ZachXBT** later pointed out that one of the deposit addresses *"was in use by a [liquidity provider] for 10 months after your July 2025 incident"* and had swept user deposits to the current hot wallet as recently as **April and May 2026**. He concluded that *"$15.8 M in user deposits was unaccounted for by your team."*

- **5:59 a.m. ET** - Gupta responded to ZachXBT, saying the team was *"investigating it further"* and that *"no user funds will be at loss."*

- **7:47 a.m. ET** - CoinDCX posted that the sweeping failure caused *"operational losses, which were absorbed by CoinDCX,"* without specifying an amount.

- ZachXBT subsequently advised users to *"withdraw all funds from CoinDCX if you value your assets,"* and described the co‑founders as having *"publicly and privately denied a theft of its assets, meaning they straight up lied or are negligently incompetent."* He also noted that he had helped the exchange freeze stolen crypto worth six figures.

---

## Context and prior incidents

- The **July 2025 hack** cost CoinDCX about **$44 million**. Bengaluru police later reported that hackers posing as recruiters tricked a CoinDCX software engineer into installing malware on a company laptop, granting the attackers internal access.

- In **March 2026**, Indian police arrested Gupta and co‑founder **Neeraj Khandelwal** over a fraud complaint that CoinDCX said stemmed from scammers impersonating the exchange via a copy‑cat site.

- **October 2025** saw **Coinbase** invest in CoinDCX in a deal Gupta said valued the exchange at **$2.45 billion**, expanding Coinbase's U.S.‑listed exchange presence in India and the Middle East.

---

## Reactions

When asked about the discrepancy between Gupta's earlier denials and the later admission, a representative for CoinDCX's public‑relations agency referred The Block to the company's blog post and did not provide additional comments.
