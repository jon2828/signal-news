MARA Holdings transferred roughly 996 BTC, worth about $81.1 million, to an address identified as belonging to Galaxy Digital, according to blockchain data flagged by Lookonchain and reported by CoinDesk.

The transfer alone does not confirm a sale, and CoinDesk is careful to say so. Coins move for plenty of reasons. But context matters, and the context here is a treasury that keeps getting smaller.

## The treasury keeps shrinking

MARA held 53,822 BTC on Feb. 26, per historical data from Bitcoin Treasuries cited by CoinDesk. By Aug. 7, that figure was 35,577 BTC. A decline of 18,245 BTC in a little over five months.

The biggest single reduction came in March, when the company sold 15,133 BTC for approximately $1.1 billion, mostly to fund the repurchase of convertible debt. Now another $81 million in coins is on the move, this time to Galaxy Digital.

I want to be careful here. Sending bitcoin to Galaxy is not the same as selling it. Custody, collateral, and a dozen other arrangements are possible. Still, when a company that already sold $1.1 billion in bitcoin this year moves another chunk to a firm like Galaxy, the obvious guess is a sale. It is only a guess, but it is not a crazy one.

## The AI pivot is doing the talking

MARA used to be a pure bitcoin miner. Per CoinDesk, it has since expanded into artificial intelligence and high-performance computing infrastructure, and it has been selling part of its treasury to strengthen the balance sheet and support that strategy.

Read that again. The bitcoin treasury is funding the AI buildout. That is the trade now: mined coins go out, data center capacity comes in.

The market, for its part, seems unbothered. MARA shares rose 2% in pre-market trading Friday to $10, though CoinDesk ties that move to bitcoin recovering above $82,000 over the past 24 hours rather than to the transfer itself.

I keep coming back to the irony. Public miners spent years pitching themselves as leveraged bitcoin exposure. Buy the stock, skip the coins, the treasury only grows. MARA's version of that pitch now comes with an asterisk: 35,577 BTC and falling, plus a bet that AI compute pays better than holding ever would.

Maybe it does. Selling bitcoin to retire convertible debt is a defensible use of the coins, and AI infrastructure revenue is real revenue if the contracts show up. But if you own MARA as a bitcoin proxy, you own something different than the pitch from a couple of years ago. You own a shrinking treasury and an AI buildout funded by it.

The next treasury update will settle whether those 996 BTC actually sold. If the number drops again, the pivot is being paid for in coins, and shareholders deserve to hear that said plainly.
